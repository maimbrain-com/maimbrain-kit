# Creator kit changelog

## 0.7.0

### Mechs and characters posed in code
- **Clip masks and additive layers:** `Node::pose_masked(clip, t, w, &mask)` and `Animator::apply_masked(node, &mask)` play a clip on some bones only, each at its own weight (`Mask::below(model, root, bone, w)` takes a whole limb or the upper body), so a walk can drive the legs while a punch drives the arms. `Skeleton::add_turn(i, rot)` turns a bone on top of this frame's clip pose (a recoil over an idle), and `Skeleton::add_clip(model, clip, t, w, mask)` adds a clip's motion on top of a pose set in code (an idle breathing at 40 % over code-driven legs). Before, a clip overrode a bone set by hand. New host calls `mb3d_anim_mask`, `mb3d_anim_take` and `mb3d_node_get_parent` (`Node::take_pose`, `Node::local`, `Node::parent`).
- **Rigs by what their bones are:** `Humanoid::find(model, root)` recognises Tripo's (`L_Thigh`, `Spine02`), Mixamo's (`mixamorig:LeftUpLeg`), Blender's (`thigh.L`, the metarig's `spine.003`), Unity-style and 3ds Max bone names and names them `hips`, `spine`, `chest`, `neck`, `head`, `upper_arm_l`, `forearm_r`, `thigh_l`, `shin_r`, `foot_l`…; `rig.turn("forearm_r", rot)`, `rig.node("hand_r")`. **`Skeleton::turn_model(i, rot)`** turns a bone in the model's frame (+y up, the model's forward) whatever axes the rig gave it, so a hand-keyed pose works on any rig; `orient`, `set_world_rot` and `world` for absolute turns.
- **IK:** `TwoBoneIk::leg(&rig, 'l')?.apply(&rig.skeleton, foot_target, forward)` plants a foot with the knee bending toward a pole direction and the foot kept flat (`flat`); `TwoBoneIk::arm` for hands; `two_bone(a, b, c, target, pole)` is the solver. `LookAt` turns a head, turret or dish toward a target within yaw and pitch limits at a limited speed.
- **Keyed poses and spring kicks that replay:** `motion::PoseKeys` keys poses (any `Lerp`: a number, `Vec3`, `Quat` or your own pose struct) on a clock you pass, such as a tell's progress 0–1, with an ease into each key, holds and a tremble (`.trembling(amount)`, read with `tremble(t, hz)`). `motion::kick(t, hz, damping)` and `Kick { at, amount, .. }.value(t)` give a spring's response as a pure function of the time since the kick (critically damped or wobbling), for strike stops, hit recoil and landing squash that match in replays at any frame rate.
- **A mech's walk:** `gfx3d::MechGait` (like `Swim` and `Flight`) plants each foot in the world and steps it toward where the body is going, forward, sideways or backward, with stride, lift and the share of time both feet are down tunable for heavy machines; the body dips after each footfall and rolls over the planted leg (`body_offset`, `body_tilt`), stopping finishes the step, and every footfall is a `Stomp { foot, pos, strength }` for dust, camera shake and foley. `apply(&rig, &legs, forward)` puts the legs on the feet with IK. The phase follows the distance walked and landings are placed at the exact moment within the frame, so replays and 120 Hz match.
- **Parts on bones:** `part.attach_to(bone)` (or `rig.attach(part, "forearm_l")`) hangs a generated part on a bone right where it is now, working out the offset from the bone, so a shield fitted by hand on the model at rest follows the arm; `attach_at(bone, &transform)` places and attaches in one.
- **mb3d-demo's `--param scene=mech`** puts it all together on a Tripo-rigged mech: a sideways walk with stomps and dust, a keyed punch with a spring stop, a hit that knocks it back, an idle clip layered over the code pose, the head tracking the camera and a briefcase in its hand. Replays match (`mb measure`).

### Fights that show on the faces
- **Portraits take damage.** `Portrait::set_health(0–1)` adds damage in stages, procedurally, so it works on any pixel-art sheet: sweat from 0.75, then soot, a cracked glass, scanlines, static bursts and a flickering red cockpit light from 0.5, then more cracks, sparks and a pulsing red alarm border with a blinking WARNING tag from 0.25. It's drawn on a grid of whole fx pixels, so it matches the art.
- **Hit reactions:** `Portrait::hit(strength)` shakes the face, flashes it white then red, knocks it aside and springs it back, tears the picture with static and shows a pained face for a moment. `Portrait::reaction(t)` has the numbers for drawing your own way. All of it is a closed-form function of game time and the portrait's seed: it replays, at any frame rate.
- Clones share the damage, so a hit on the game's copy shows in the dialogue box and barks; the player's own HUD portrait works the same way.
- **Damage rows:** `mb art portrait --expressions …,hurt,battered` spells those rows out (wincing; bruised, sooty, scratched, a cracked visor), and a portrait with rows named `hurt` and `battered` uses them for hits and low health (`.damage_faces` renames them).
- The UI gallery's DAMAGE page beats a portrait from full health to near death.
- **Faces as large as the box allows:** `Portrait::draw` sizes a face so each *art* pixel is a whole number of device pixels, using how many sheet pixels make one, instead of rounding the sheet's scale down to half units. An `mb art portrait` sheet (192-pixel cells, 48 art pixels across) now fills a 240-unit box (it drew 192), comes out 120 in the dialogue box's 132 (it was 96), and fits an 84-unit HUD corner at 72 (it drew 96, over the edge). `.art_pixels(n)` sets the grid; a cell that's a multiple of 48 is taken as 48 art pixels, anything else as one sheet pixel each, as before. `mb art portrait` prints the `.art_pixels` call for its grid.
- **`Portrait::clipped()`** keeps a hit's knock and shake, the static and the damage overlays inside the rect the face is drawn in, so a game needn't leave room around a framed portrait. mb2d has no scissor, so the SDK cuts the sprites and overlay cells to the rect (`Rect::intersect`); the alarm border is the frame. The gallery's DAMAGE page clips both faces.

### Cutscenes for intros and endings
- **`ui::Sequence`** plays a cutscene as a timeline: `shot` (cut), `move_to` (camera moves with easing), `line` (voiced lines with portraits and subtitles, on `Dialogue`), `wait`, `fade_in`/`fade_out`, `letterbox`, `title`, `credits`, `sound` and `cue` (a `SeqEvent::Cue` for the game's part), with `.meanwhile()` to run a step alongside the next. Each step starts the moment the last one ends, so it's a function of game time and lands the same at any frame rate. `camera()` gives a `gfx3d::Camera` to draw with.
- A tap hurries a line or cuts a step short; holding a finger 0.7 s skips the rest, with the camera, bars and fade where the scene would have left them and every cue still reported.
- **A cutscene box in the game's own style:** `Sequence::draw_line(|l| ..)` (and `Dialogue::draw_line`) draws the lines' box your own way. Each frame it gets a `LineBox`: where the default box would be (above the letterbox bar, sliding up as it opens), the line (speaker, expression, text, how much is out, mouth), the speaker's portrait, the theme and the time; `l.shown()` is the text typed so far and `l.draw_text(..)` types it out wrapped as the whole line will be. Without it the box is the theme's panel as before; `seq.talk.theme` gives it another theme's.
- The skill's engagement.md has "Writing an ending": the defeat, the cost, the hero's reaction, the world after with a closing line, credits with the cast, in 45–60 s. The UI gallery's ENDING page plays a 20 s one.

### A stats card that reads on any theme, with sound
- **`StatsCard` text reads on any theme:** a color that wouldn't read on the card's panel over the scrim (dark ink on a dark panel came out navy on navy) is drawn near-white or near-black instead; `.ink(color)` picks the numbers' color yourself. New color helpers `ui::contrast`, `ui::readable` and `ui::over`.
- **The teaser's line never runs into "TAP TO CONTINUE":** it shrinks to fit, then wraps, the panel grows to hold it, and the prompt keeps below it.
- **`StatsCard::sounds(CardSounds)`** plays a jingle as the card comes in, a swish per row, blips as the numbers roll (pitched up as they go), a thunk as each lands, the rank's stamp, whooshes for the teaser and the wipe, and a sting out. `CardSounds::load("assets/sfx")` takes the eight files the skill's new `scripts/card_sfx.py` writes (sfx.py, free, in the music's key with `--key`). UI.md has how to bring the fight music down under the card and the next fight's in with the wipe (`Director::stop`, `restart`, or `go(.., Change::Stinger)` on `Teaser`).
### Sound and story for a voiced duel
- **Foley from motion (`maimbrain::foley`):** every moving thing can make sound without wiring each event. `Gait` steps each foot from a stride's phase (any frame length), `Contact` lands a foot or a body from its height and gives the speed it came down at, `Drive::servo`/`Drive::thruster` are loops whose level and pitch follow a joint's speed or a thrust (frame-rate independent attack and release; silent when still), and `Foley::step`/`impact`/`hit` play one-shots as hard as the motion was. Everything is placed from the camera (`Listener::from_camera`; `Space`: −6 dB per doubling past 4 m, silent past 60 m, panned to its side), varied per play from its own seed (replays hear the same), a burst of one sound from one place folded into one, and at most six one-shots at once with the loudest heard winning. `foley::Log` writes a `mix_check.py` log; `examples/mech_foley.rs` is two mechs walking, turning, hopping, landing, trading punches and losing an arm. mb3d-demo's retro scene plays it live.
- **A mech's sound set in one command:** `mb sfx <game> --pack mech [--prefix P] [--flavor "a rusty junkyard robot"]` generates stomp, servo and thruster loops, hydraulic hiss, metal impact and a part tearing off (about $0.01 on ElevenLabs), and the skill's `sfx.py --pack mech <dir>` makes the same set procedurally, free. assets.md §5 has "every moving thing makes sound", engagement.md §8 and its checklist the rule.
- **A different song per zone, on purpose:** `mb music <game> <name> ["<shared prompt>"] --zone harbor="bluesy hard rock, 128 bpm" --zone neon="flamenco-tango, 112 bpm"` makes each zone its own piece in its own genre, key and tempo (a "N bpm" in its prompt is asked for), each with its own music graph (16 bars by default), stinger, swell and tension kit, and `<name>.songs.json` listing them with the tempo and key each came out in. No key check runs between them; `--zones` (one piece) keeps it, and its key warning now points at `--zone` for zones meant to differ. **`music::Songs`** plays them, a `Director` per song: `go` changes at the playing song's next exit cue, the old song dying away under the next song's stinger from half a beat before the bar line and the next starting from its top on it (`Change::NextBar`, `Change::Crossfade(bars)` too); `Setlist::parse` reads the list; `Director::start_at` and `Director::next_exit` are public for games that schedule their own; `music::Log::with_ids_after` lets logs merge. `examples/songs_switch.rs` renders a change.
  - **Zones one run at a time, and a failure doesn't stop the rest:** `<name>.songs.json` is merged, not rewritten (a zone made again replaces its entry), and a single `--zone` works. A zone that fails is reported and the zones after it still generate; the run ends with a summary, exits non-zero and prints the command that makes each failed zone again on its own. A content-filter refusal names the prompt's words that likely tripped it ("stomping", "greedy swagger") and the retry has plainer ones; ElevenLabs' suggested rewrite is shown when it sends one. A take no loop can be cut from is asked again while `--retries` last, then once more with Lyria 3.5 (gemini) or half the bars, and a 30 s clip model gets 12 or 8 bars instead of 16 when 16 don't fit. The mock provider refuses a prompt containing `mock-refuse`, to try it for free.
- **Writing a story a player can follow** (assets.md "Voice and characters", engagement.md §9 and its checklist):
  - **No inside baseball:** a budget of about five proper nouns per game and one new one per scene, every name on screen before it's spoken, things described by what the player sees ("the big guy with the crane"), and the "could a 10-year-old retell this?" check.
  - **Cartoon slapstick and puns** that land in a two-second bark: the setup on screen, the pun as the last word, physical comedy the mech or portrait shows, an escalating gag, with before/after examples.
  - **A villain turn** that's clear and final: a plain evil want, a cruel choice on screen, no last-second redemption, one early line to set it up and a clean beat for the hero. The all-ages writing rules are unchanged.
### Seeing the game clearly
- **Textures stay sharp at a slant:** mb3d filters textures 16× anisotropically (was 4×), so a floor going into the distance or a mech's flank seen at an angle keeps its detail instead of going to a blurrier mip. On by default, for every mipmapped texture; on the desktop preview's GPU it cost nothing measurable. In mb3d-demo's `--param scene=hero --param view=grazing` (a low camera along a steel deck), the far deck's edge energy is 27% higher and its tread pattern shows where it was a smear. Browsers clamp it to what the GPU has (16 on iPhones).
- **`Post::sharpen`** (0–1, off by default) sharpens the final image: an unsharp mask on lightness that keeps within the neighbouring pixels' range, so edges don't get halos. 0.3–0.5 is mild: `gfx3d::post(&Post { sharpen: 0.4, ..Lighting::hero().post })`. 4 extra texture reads per pixel, not measurable on the desktop preview; not with the retro look. It replaces `Post`'s `_reserved` field: a game that wrote `_reserved: 0.0` writes `sharpen: 0.0`.
- **Texture sizes for generated models:** `mb model --help`, the skill, assets.md and GENERATE.md say when 1024² is worth it (the hero, anything seen close: its texture is its detail) and when 512 or 256 is enough (mid-size things, small props, crowds), and that `--reprocess --texture-size N` changes it for free. `mb build`'s texture-budget and bundle-size warnings now say to shrink props and far models first and keep the hero at 1024, instead of suggesting 512 for every model (Bolt Brawl's mechs had been cut to 256²).
- **When the retro look hurts:** the skill and MB3D.md now say when not to use it (small subjects, dark scenes, textured generated models), what to keep instead (full resolution with crisp edges, a rim and outline, a lit scene, the period flavor in the HUD and backdrops), and that toon bands flatten a generated model's texture to two or three tones. `mb shot --readability` names the retro look as a likely cause when it's on and the subject doesn't read.
- **`Lighting::hero()`** lights a textured model against a bright sky in one call (`gfx3d::lighting(&Lighting::hero().key_from(cam.rot.forward()))`): a clear day atmosphere, which is the fill light and what metal reflects, a warm key from over the camera's shoulder with shadows, exposure 1, little bloom. **`MaterialStyle::hero()`** is smooth shading with a rim and a thin outline. mb3d already lit textured PBR from the sky (SH fill, prefiltered reflections) and rimmed smooth materials; what was missing was the setup, so it's SDK only. mb3d-demo's `--param scene=hero` shows the generated dragon lit that way, and `look=muddy` the same model lit like Bolt Brawl. The skill's "Lighting recipes" and MB3D.md have the recipe.
- **`mb shot --readability`** measures whether the subject reads, after a player found Bolt Brawl's mechs "really hard to see" and nothing had warned the builder. Name the subject with `node.mark_subject()` (or `--subject <handle>`); under each shot it prints the share of the screen it covers, the lightness contrast (CIE L*, what greyscale keeps) and color contrast of its outline against what's right around it, its own tonal range, how much of it the HUD covers and the frame's lightness range, warns when it doesn't separate, is tiny, flat, under the HUD or the frame is dark, and says when the retro look may be the cause. `<shot>.readability.png` shows the frame in greyscale with the subject outlined. The silhouette comes from redrawing the frame with and without the subject (`mb.isolate`, presentation only; `mb.stats()` reports `retro`). Calibrated on mb3d-demo: the hero scene, koi pond and retro mechs pass (outline ΔL* 14–35, half or more of it ≥ 15); the same dragon lit like Bolt Brawl fails (ΔL* 3, 15%, frame L* 1–5). mb3d-demo marks its ship, first koi, blue mech and dragon.
- **`mb shot --at mark:<label>[+SECS]`** shoots a moment instead of a time: the frame where the game first calls `sys::mark("<label>")` (the boss arrives, a part breaks), or SECS after it, mixed freely with plain times and in `--sheet` lists (`--at 2,mark:boss,mark:boss+0.5`). It waits up to `--mark-wait` seconds (60) and otherwise fails naming the labels the game did mark. Files are `shot-boss.png`, `shot-boss+0.5.png`; `mb.step(n, { until: ["boss"] })` stops after such a frame. mb3d-demo marks its explosions (`--at mark:boom`).

### Aim by pointing the phone
- **A new `attitude` sensor:** declare `sensors = ["attitude"]` and `sensors::attitude()` gives the phone's orientation (a quaternion in the game's axes) and its rotation rate, from the gyroscope at 100 Hz, so turning the phone left and right is visible (tilt only sees gravity). No permission prompt; the heading is relative and drifts slowly, so measure from a reference: `Attitude::relative_to`, `yaw()`, `pitch()`, `roll()`. Recorded in replays like tilt.
- **`sensors::Scope`** turns pointing into a crosshair the way a light gun should feel. `Scope::new` is the simple light-gun mapping, which a playtest on an iPhone preferred: 32° of turn across the screen, every degree the same at any speed, a light fixed smoothing (8 ms) and a 0.1° dead zone, with recentering on demand and slow drift correction while the aim rests near the center, edges that hold with a little give, and fallbacks to tilt or dragging without a gyroscope. The skill's motion controls have the recipe ("pointing to aim"). **`Scope::precise`** turns on the standard gyro-aim precision techniques, for games about fine aiming at small targets (each is also a field to set on its own):
  - **Speed-adaptive smoothing** (`Smoothing::PRECISE`, a One Euro filter): a resting hand's tremor is cut to about a third, while a 60°/s swing trails by ~5 ms. `Smoothing::Fixed(secs)` is the plain fixed filter (the default, 0.008).
  - **A precision zone** (`GainCurve::PRECISE`, with 40° across at full gain): slow turns (≤ 10°/s) get ×0.6 of the gain, easing to the full gain at 50°/s, so homing in is finer and swings are as long as ever. `GainCurve::FLAT` (the default) turns it off.
  - **Friction on targets** (`scope.set_targets(&[Target::new(x, y, r)])`, `scope.friction`, 0.4 in `precise`, 0 by default): the gain drops to ×0.6 over a target, so swings stop on it; the crosshair never moves by itself. `scope.magnetism` (off in both) adds a gentle pull in proportion to the hand's movement.
  - `gain_scale()`, `time_constant()` and `speed()` for a readout. The UI gallery's AIM page shows them and toggles DEFAULT/PRECISE to compare the two.
- **`input::synth::aim`** is a hand swinging onto a target (overshoot, settle, tremor) as attitude samples, for bots and tests.
- **`mb shot` / `mb measure --aim YAW[,PITCH]@T[:SECS]`** points the phone in a script, at once or turning there like a hand (minimum jerk, sampled at 100 Hz with the rotation rate a gyro reports), recorded like real samples so `mb.verify()` replays it.
- **The preview emulates it from the mouse:** across the window turns the phone, up and down points it, ±45° at the edges. `?sensors=none` and `mb.sensors(null)` switch it off.

### Generated creatures that move
- **`mb model --spine`** gives any generated model code-driven bones: joints head to tail along its body with smooth weights, and two-bone wings for birds. It replaces a rig mb3d can't use (no rig, over 64 joints, one wing, a clip that coils the body). `--import file.glb` runs it, free, on a model you already have.
- **`Skeleton::spine(node)`** adopts those bones right after `spawn` (`Skeleton::adopt` takes any model's bones by name), so `Swim` and `Wave` bend a generated dragon or fish like the koi built in code.
- **`gfx3d::Flight`** flies a bird: wingbeats from its climb and speed, glides, banking into turns, a bob, a level head, and `Strike` (rear back, dive with wings swept, flare). `gfx3d::alive` has breathing, anticipation and look-at helpers; the skill has the recipe "a creature that feels alive".
- **Safer rigs:** a skin over 64 joints loses leaf joints instead of drawing unskinned; one-sided rigs are called out; the sidecar has the joint tree.
- **Facing:** a long creature that came back backwards (its thicker end behind) is turned round, or flagged "facing uncertain".
- **Stable names:** the nodes a generated model draws with are `<asset>/body`, `<asset>/body2`…, the same in every take.

### Rigging and animating generated models (`mb model`)
- **Rig a model made earlier:** `mb model <game> <name> --rig [--animate idle,slash]` with no prompt rigs `assets/<name>.glb` from the generation task its sidecar recorded (Tripo, $0.25, no new mesh), or from an upload of its raw `.glb` when there's no task. The rig task is recorded (`result.rig_task`), so a later `--animate hit_to_body_01` adds clips to the same rig ($0.10 each) without rigging again, and a clip it already has isn't made twice. The model is reprocessed with its whole recipe, which now has the rig and clips, so `--reprocess` keeps them; the unrigged download is kept beside the rigged one. `--rig-task <id>` adopts a rig made elsewhere. (Bolt Brawl's prototype paid for a second rig because the first run's task id wasn't saved.)
- **`mb model presets`** lists Tripo's real clip names (101 `preset:biped:*`, and the other rig types' one each) with what real runs showed: `chop` is the same motion as `slash`, `punch`/`kick`/`defeat_01`/`strafe` aren't presets (and what to ask for instead), the lengths (Idle 15.4 s, Hurt 13.9 s mostly filler) and where Slash's strike is. `--animate` with a name that isn't a Tripo preset is refused before anything is spent.
- **Clip trimming:** `mb model --clip Idle=0..4,Slash=0.6..3.4` keeps only the part of a clip you use; `Strike=Slash@1.8..3.4` cuts a new clip from part of one (several beats from one clip), `Swing=Slash` renames and `Chop=drop` drops. Recorded in the recipe, so `--reprocess` and `mb regen` repeat it; a later `--clip` replaces the edit for the same clip, `--clip none` clears them.
- **Armor that doesn't stretch:** `mb model --rigid [all|arms,legs|<joints>]` gives each vertex its single strongest bone, so a mech's plates move as plates instead of bending like skin; `--pin cape=spine` (also `skirt=hips`, `hanging=<bone>`, `auto`) moves what hangs off a limb (farther from its bones than the limb is thick, behind the body or below the hips) onto the body, the spine chain by height, blended over a band so there's no crease. Bolt Brawl's SWORDMASTER's heel-length cape, which Tripo tied to the calves, thighs and forearms, stopped stretching on the slash: 65 % of its edges stretched or squashed past 25 % over the clips before, 5 % after. The run reports that, writes `art/preview/<name>.weights.png` (each clip before and after), and records both in the recipe.
- **Facing from the rig:** a rigged biped faces where its rig says: its toes forward of its ankles and its `L_`/`R_` sides (forward = up × right). Bolt Brawl's mechs, squat enough that mb said "facing uncertain: not a long shape", are settled (`facing_check.verdict: rig`); Tripo's rigged mechs come back facing +x, not +z, so without a `--turn` mb now turns them and says so (`--turn 360` keeps the old way, for a game that turns them in code, as Bolt Brawl's `BODY_YAW` does); with a `--turn` it warns if the rig ends up facing elsewhere. `mb model info` prints the rig's forward.
- **The rig symmetry check reads side names:** joints named `L_`/`R_` (Tripo), `Left…`/`Right…` (Mixamo), `.L`/`.R` or `_l`/`_r` are paired by name, so a symmetric biped no longer gets "38 on the right have no mirror" (Tripo's mechs face +x, so their sides are ±z and the old check, which only mirrored positions across x, misfired). A rig whose names don't say is still checked by position.

### Retro look (mb3d 2)
- **`gfx3d::retro(&Retro::saturn())`** draws the 3D scene at a low resolution (`resolution: (320, 240)` gives 240 square pixels across a portrait phone, each a whole number of device pixels), blows it up with nearest-neighbour pixels with hard (unantialiased) edges, cuts colors to 15-bit (`color_bits: 5`) with an ordered Bayer `dither`, snaps vertices to the pixel grid (`vertex_snap`, the PS1/Saturn wobble) and bands the fog (`fog_bands`). The mb2d HUD stays at full resolution, so pixel-art HUDs and portraits stay crisp. Presets `Retro::saturn/ps1/arcade/snes`. It costs less than full resolution.
- **`Texture::nearest()`** samples a texture nearest-neighbour from its full-size image (no mipmaps, no blur), for pixel art on 3D surfaces; **`Model::pixelated()`** does it for every texture of a loaded .glb.
- **`gfx3d::backdrop(&[Layer])`** draws up to 4 parallax layers behind the 3D (after the sky), wrapped around the camera and turning at their own rates as it yaws and pitches (`Layer::new(t).scroll(0.45)`: far mountains slow, near hills faster), pixel-crisp.
- **`MeshData::mode7_floor(radius, tile, segments)`** is a big tiled ground plane with UVs in world units over `tile` (a Mode 7 floor with a nearest texture and an unlit material), and **`gfx3d::mode7_anchor`** keeps it under the camera snapped to whole tiles, so it never runs out or slides; fog in the backdrop's bottom color fades it into the horizon.
- mb3d-demo's `--param scene=retro` puts them together: two blocky mechs on a Mode 7 floor under a banded dusk sky with parallax mountains and hills, Saturn look on (tap to compare); the look renders 25% faster than full resolution there.

### Bigger games in a bundle
- **The portrait splitter warns only on real problems:** a sheet whose busts touch falls back to the even grid without a warning unless the grid cuts through heads, and an uneven background is only reported when some of it was left behind along the edges (with the `--tolerance` to try). On Bolt Brawl's seven portrait sheets the warnings went from six to the one sheet that has magenta left on it.
- **A voice line with missing files still has a subtitle:** `VoiceLine::text_if_missing(text)` (which `Barks::load_script` calls with each script line's text, audio tags left out) shows the text, typed out with a flapping mouth, when the line's timing doesn't load, and a missing `.ogg` logs a warning and plays the subtitle alone.
- **`mb model` checks the Tripo balance first:** it reads the account's spendable credits before generating and refuses a run the balance can't cover, with the shortfall in dollars and credits, instead of failing with "Insufficient credits" partway through a batch.
- **Gemini and Vertex sidecars record what was spent:** `cost_usd` was null because Google doesn't report a charge; it's now the list price of what was billed (the image at the size actually asked for, or the music call), so `mb art check`'s total is right.
- **`mb art sky` finds soft horizons:** the finder looks for the row where the picture changes most (brightness falling, a thin line where the averages hardly move, saturation changing) with a prior around 55%, instead of the sharpest brightness drop in the middle third, which on a sunset over the sea landed in the water. It records `horizon_confidence` and warns when it's a guess. On Bolt Brawl's four skies it lands within 2% of the horizons the builder set by hand.
- **`mb art layers` keeps the ground and keys the sky:** a nearer layer is keyed from its top rows and the key color it was asked for (its scenery covers the bottom and sides, so the border's median keyed nothing), cropped with its bottom at the lowest solid row instead of from the center, and its last rows made solid for `gfx3d::backdrop`. The far layer stays opaque by design; the nearer ones are told it's a palette and light reference, not a subject to redraw, and the sidecar records each layer's index, transparency and crop.
- **Keying finds the background the model was asked for, in one tone or two:** keying looks for the key color the prompt asked for (recorded as the recipe's `key`; older sidecars' prompts are read for it) instead of trusting the border's median, and keys a second flat tone close to it (a two-tone magenta) too, recording `keyed_background_2`. `mb regen --reprocess` takes `--tolerance` and `--key '#rrggbb'`.
- **Provenance stays out of the bundle:** generation sidecars are for `mb regen`, `mb art check` and audits, not the game, so `mb build` leaves them out of the `.mbx` and says how many and how big (Bolt Brawl: 199 sidecars, 0.52 MB, 63 KB of the compressed 10 MB, and one entry). They stay in the game's folder. Code that names a `.gen.json` still ships them as they are; code that names `provenance.json` gets them packed into one `assets/provenance.json`.
- **A voiced line costs one bundle entry, not three.** `mb build` leaves the provenance sidecars out (above) and packs each character's lip timings into one `assets/vo/<character>/lips.txt` (a lips pack); the game's folder keeps its per-file sidecars and `.lips.txt` files. `VoiceLine::load` reads the pack and falls back to the per-line file, so `mb dev` and older bundles work as before; a game on an older SDK keeps per-line timings (`mb build` says so). A 120-line voiced game goes from ~500 of the 512 entries to ~270.
- **`mb build`'s "maybe unused" list stops flooding:** names built at runtime count as uses (a string literal's pattern, `format!("assets/vo/{}/{}", ch, id)` or `"bd_"`, and each row of a voice script in `art/*.csv`), it names at most the 15 biggest with a count of the rest, leaves out the files `mb build` packs, and shows only when the bundle is near a size or entry limit. A voiced 3D game's list went from 189 assets to the 9 models it really doesn't load.
- **Texture estimates that know about scenes:** `art/budget.toml` lists what each scene loads (`[load] always = [...]`, `harbor = ["sky_harbor.jpg", "bd_harbor_*.png"]`), and `mb build` checks the biggest scene against mb3d's 96 MB and 64 textures, with the all-at-once number as a second line. A game over the all-at-once budget whose code frees textures is pointed at it.
- **`mb build` warns from 80% of the 512 entries** with a count by kind (voice lines, lip timings, images, sounds, sources…), instead of the validator failing at 513. The 512 stays (SPEC §1 says why).

### Looks (mb3d 2)
- **Tint or brighten a generated model without regenerating it:** `Model::tint([r, g, b])` multiplies every material's base color (maps kept), `Model::brighten(k)` makes it glow with `k` of its own colors (its color map becomes its emissive), and `Material::tint`, `Material::brighten` and `Material::get` do it per material. `Model::materials()` lists a model's materials and `Model::node_names()` its nodes (what `Node::find` takes). New imports `mb3d_material_get`, `mb3d_model_materials`, `mb3d_model_node_names`; games that don't call them render exactly as before.
- **Sky v2:**
  - `gfx3d::atmosphere` adds a zenith-to-horizon gradient with a horizon glow band and a halo around the sun or moon.
  - `gfx3d::clouds` adds drifting clouds lit by the sun or moon, with a silver lining.
  - `gfx3d::sky_disc` draws a real sun or moon disc, with limb darkening, seas and a corona.
  - `gfx3d::sky_texture` shows a painted equirectangular panorama.
  - Presets: `Atmosphere::night/dusk/day`, `Clouds::night/dusk/day`, `SkyDisc::moon/sun`.
  - `mb art sky` generates a seamless panorama for it.
- **Water v2:** `gfx3d::water(&Water)` makes the reflector's water look like water instead of a rippled mirror.
  - Small ripples carried by a current (`flow`, `flow_speed`, `detail`), fading into a glitter path with distance instead of shimmering.
  - A body you see into (`depth_fade`): fish, the bed and posts under the surface, bent by the ripples and fading into the water's color with depth; light glowing inside it under lanterns and toward a low sun (`tint`, `scatter`).
  - Foam where it meets walls, rocks, boats and fish (`foam`), streaming with the current; wake and crest foam takes its color.
  - Glints of the sun, moon and point lights (`sparkle`), and caustics on things under the water and on walls just above it (`caustics`).
  - Presets: `Water::canal()`, `Water::lake()`, `Water::pond()`, and `.flowing(dir, speed)`. Games that don't call it look exactly as before.
  - The current can change every frame (say, to run down whichever canal the player is in): the ripples carry on from where they are instead of jumping by the change × the time since the session began. A current that never changes draws exactly as before.
  - `mb.stats()` has `under`: the draws of what's under the water.
- **Distance fade, so nothing pops:**
  - `node.set_fade(start, end)` dithers a node out between `start` and `end` meters from the camera and stops drawing it beyond: it isn't drawn, counted, cast or reflected.
  - Each instance of an instanced node fades on its own, so a row of houses fades house by house.
  - Children inherit the fade, `clear_fade()` undoes it and `never_fade()` opts out (the player, landmarks).
  - `gfx3d::draw_distance(start, end)` gives every small node a default fade, and `Fog::draw_distance()` picks one inside the fog, so things come out of the mist. MB3D.md: put cull distances inside the fog; fade, never pop.
  - The dither is a stable per-pixel screen door (blended materials fade their alpha) and costs nothing for draws that aren't fading. New imports `mb3d_node_fade`, `mb3d_draw_distance`. Games that don't call them render pixel for pixel as before.
- **Water that reacts:** on the reflector's water:
  - `gfx3d::ripple` starts ring ripples.
  - `gfx3d::wake` trails a V wake and bow wave behind anything moving.
  - `Splash` gives crown, droplets, mist and spray presets, and starts the rings.
  - `Ring::height` lets floating things bob on the rings.
- **The beauty pass:** the last layer of polish, each off until called:
  - `gfx3d::grade` grades the final image: lift/gamma/gain per channel, split toning, vibrance, an S-curve contrast with a filmic toe and shoulder, and a 3D LUT baked in any photo editor (`tools/lut.py`, new in 3D games, makes, stamps and extracts the LUT strip). Presets: `Grade::moonlit/golden_hour/neon`, and `Grade::lerp`.
  - `gfx3d::shafts` casts light shafts (god rays) from the sun or moon through whatever stands in front of it, thickest in the fog (`LightShafts::moonbeams/sunbeams`).
  - `gfx3d::halos` makes point lights glow in the air around them, cut off by what's in front (`Halos::mist`).
  - `gfx3d::film` adds film grain that moves with game time and anamorphic streaks off the brightest points (`Film::subtle`).
  - The skill's "Beauty pass" says in what order to tune them and how to keep the course readable. The moonlit canal and Mistwood use them.
- **Organic meshes built in code:**
  - `MeshData::loft` sweeps a shape along a `Spline`.
  - `subdivide` and `smooth_normals` turn a coarse shape smooth.
  - `MeshData::fish` is a worked example.
- **Bones for meshes built in code:**
  - `Skeleton::chain` with `Node::set_skin` gives a code-built mesh bones.
  - Things ride a code-built creature's bones: `Skeleton::bone(i)`, `tip()`, `find(name)` (after `with_names`) give a bone's node to parent to, and `socket(i, point)` makes a node on a bone at a point of the mesh's rest pose (eyes, a lantern, a light).
  - `Wave` and `Swim` give creatures, banners and tassels a traveling-wave motion that follows speed and is the same at any frame rate.
- **The 3D examples the docs point to ship in the kit:** `examples/mistwood` and `examples/mb3d-demo`, including the moonlit canal (sky, water, the beauty pass) and the koi pond (lofts, bones, clear water).

### Generated parts that fit (`mb model`)
- **Parts don't get character style:** `mb model --kind part|prop|vehicle` sends the style guide's new `[3d_object]` words and the palette instead of the `[3d]` words, `style` and `keywords` (written for characters: "anime mecha", "visible mechanical joints" turned guns into little robots), says it's one hard-surface object, and puts "character, robot, face, arms, legs…" in the negative prompt. Without `--kind` it's guessed from the prompt and printed; a part is centered on the origin. Recorded in the recipe.
- **Glow by color:** `mb model --glow-color '#5bca2b[,#ff8020]' [--glow-tolerance 0.15]` lights only the texels near those colors (a gem, a core, coolant stripes), where `--glow` lights by brightness and so lights a white or yellow part all over. The run prints the share lit and warns at none or too much; it's in the recipe, so `--reprocess` and `mb regen` repeat it (`--no-glow` drops it). No mask script to rerun after a reprocess.
- **Compressed geometry by default:** `mb model` and `--reprocess` write the geometry quantized (`KHR_mesh_quantization`: 16-bit positions, 8-bit normals, 16-bit UVs and weights, 16-bit rotation keys), which mb3d reads on the web and on iOS. About half the geometry bytes, no visible change: Bolt Brawl's hauler mech went from 1105 to 931 KB (geometry 391 → 213 KB), where the bundle's deflate saved little on floats. The run prints both sizes; `--no-compress` writes floats. The node drawing a compressed mesh carries its dequantizing offset and scale: move parts by a node above it.
- **Smaller maps, matte metal:** `--maps-size N` sizes the normal and metal-rough maps apart from the base color, and defaults to half of `--texture-size` (Bolt Brawl's hauler: 931 → 595 KB with 1024² color and 512² maps). A model made before gets half-size maps the next time it's reprocessed (pass `--maps-size` to choose). `--metal 0.15 --min-roughness 0.6` mattes a chrome-like model (the roughness floor is baked into the metal-rough map, which a material's roughness factor can't raise), and mb warns when a model comes back as polished metal, which reads near-black outdoors.
- **`--reprocess` keeps everything and stays put:** it reapplies the whole recorded recipe (kind, glow colors, compression, map sizes, matte) with only the options given changed, writes to `--out` (the model it reads) instead of the path the model was first made at, and `mb regen --reprocess` on a model moved since it was made writes it where it is now. Before, a part made into `art/pending/` and moved to `assets/` was written back to `art/pending/`.
- **Parts in a stated pose:** `mb model --orient tip=+y` (blades, barrels: the slim end up, the flat side to +z), `tip=+z up=+y` (guns: muzzle forward) or `face=+z` (plates, shields: the outer face, told by its glow or its bulge) turns a part by its shape, wherever the generator posed it; Bolt Brawl's beam saber, generated along a diagonal, stands straight up. A `--kind part` prompt also asks for a plain rest pose to match. **`mb model info <glb>`** prints a model's bounds, principal axes, tip, face, glow spot, anchor and socket offset, and the `--orient` that suits it (what the builder's `socket_fit.py` worked out by hand).

### Generated assets
- **A network blip doesn't fail a generation, and nothing is paid for twice:** every provider call (`mb art`, `music`, `sfx`, `voice`, `model`, Maimbrain's keys) tries up to 4 times, 1, 2 and 4 s apart (or the provider's `Retry-After`). A POST is sent again only when the provider can't have run it: the connection failed before the whole request went out (mb counts the body as it's sent), or the answer was 429 or 503. A connection that drops after the request went out isn't retried, and the error says the provider may have billed it. Polling and downloads also retry a dropped connection and 500/502/504. `MB_HTTP_TRIES` sets the tries. Voice had no retrying either; its slot logic is unchanged.
- **`mb model` lists the nodes it wrote** (`nodes: heron2/body, heron2/body2 (spawn.find("heron2/body") …)`, `mesh_nodes` in the sidecar), and says `facing uncertain` for squat or round shapes instead of nothing. `--spine` no longer warns about merging joints of the rig it replaces. GENERATE.md: describe what you want positively (generators read "NO arms" as arms; use `--negative`).
- **Parallel `mb model` runs queue instead of failing:** Tripo runs 5 P-series (10 H-series) generation tasks at once per account and refuses one more (429, code 2000). `mb model` now takes one of the pool's slots (a lock file in mb's config directory) before starting, so a sixth parallel run waits for one to finish, and retries a refusal with backoff (5 s up to 60 s, within `MB_MODEL_TIMEOUT`). GENERATE.md documents the limits.
- **`mb model`:** generated 3D models (`.glb`) from a prompt or a reference image.
  - Rigging and animation clips (swim, walk, run, idle, …) are optional.
  - Output is fitted to the platform: polycount, size and facing, textures, glow.
  - A turntable preview sheet is written alongside.
  - Provider: Tripo (default) or Meshy, on your own key (`mb keys set tripo`).
- **`mb art sky`:** see Sky v2 above.
- **`mb art portrait`:** a talking-portrait sheet for `ui::Portrait`: 4 mouth shapes (closed to wide open) × a row per expression (`--expressions smug,angry,scared`), plus a blink row. The cells are aligned so the head holds still, and the layout is recorded in the sidecar.

### Motion and camera
- **`Follow::jump_target`:** a target that jumps (a creature's next phase, a new waypoint) was read as one frame of very fast motion, so the path after it differed at 60 and 120 Hz (`--smoothness` flagged position jumps); call it in the frame the target jumps and the value glides over the same way at any rate. The docs say when to use it and when `snap`.
- **Springs:** `motion::Follow` is a critically damped spring that follows a moving target on the same path at any frame rate.
- **Spring lag:** `Follow::lag_for(halflife, speed)` says how far a spring trails a moving target without one at hand, and `motion::HALFLIFE_W` is public.
- **Camera:** `gfx3d::SpringCamera` follows without kinks. `FollowCamera` is first-order smoothing, kept for existing games.
- **Guidance:** the skill's "Smooth motion" section covers anything the camera follows.

### Tooling
- **`mb build` warns about characters a font lacks** (they draw as `?`): it reads the text the game ships (`art/*.csv` voice scripts, string and char literals in `src/*.rs` outside comments and tests, `src/*.txt`), checks it against the fonts the game draws with (Inter; the pixel font when the code names it; each baked font with a full alphabet, not a digits-only score face), and names each character once with up to three `file:line` places and the fix: the `chars` to set in fonts.toml (`ascii` widened to `latin1`, anything outside it added literally), a stale atlas to re-bake, or emoji and characters no built-in font has to replace:
  ```
  warning: 2 characters in the game's text aren't in a font it draws with, so they draw as "?":
    é (U+00E9) in art/lines.csv:2, src/lib.rs:88: not in eb-garamond
    😵 (U+1F635) in art/lines.csv:3: not in Inter, eb-garamond
    fix: set chars = "latin1" for eb-garamond in fonts.toml (`mb build` re-bakes it)
    fix: fonts have no emoji like 😵: replace them with words, or draw them as images
  ```
- **`mb build` near a size limit says what to cut:** a bundle over or within 15% of 10 MiB lists its biggest assets and the ones no string in the game's source names ("maybe unused", e.g. models a rework retired), with a hint to move them out of `assets/`; a startup set near 3 MiB lists its biggest startup assets, and wasm near 4 MiB says so.
- **Several `mb shot`s on one game folder at once work:** `mb shot`, `mb measure`, `mb serve` and `mb build` take a per-game build lock from re-baking fonts until the wasm is copied out (`another mb run is building games/brawl: waiting for it, then using its build`), so one run's cargo or wasm-opt can't rewrite the wasm another is reading; the ones that waited find the build up to date and reuse it. Four parallel `mb shot games/mb3d-demo` after an edit: one build, three short waits, four shots.
- **`mb build`'s "maybe unused" list reads index files:** what a JSON asset the game names lists counts as used, followed through, so a setlist's music graphs (`<name>.songs.json`), each graph's take, layers, stingers, swell and tension kit, and the files any other JSON index names (as `assets/…` or next to it) are no longer flagged. Bolt Brawl's `--zone` songs had all been listed as maybe unused.
- **`mb measure` says when the browser, not the game, set the frame rate:** a run whose frames came at 30 fps while the game used a few ms of the 16.7 it has gets a `pacing` line ("the browser paced frames at 30.0 fps, not the 60 Hz asked for: the game used ~5.9 ms a frame (CPU p95 1.30 + GPU 4.60) … so not the game's fault") naming what it saw on the machine (on battery, Low Power Mode, other headless browsers); `--json` has `browserPaced`. Its browser profile turns Chrome's Energy Saver off, which capped pages at 30 fps on battery.
- **Going over the texture budget is never silent:** a texture or model that doesn't fit mb3d's 96 MB / 64 textures still returns −4, and now the page logs why, once per asset (`runtime: assets/sky.jpg refused (−4): a 2048×1024 texture needs 10.7 MB …; 90.1 of the 96 MB texture budget is in use (14 textures) …`; `mb shot` and `mb measure` print it). `Texture::try_new` and `Model::try_load` return the error code. `mb.stats()` has `textureBytes`, `textures`, `textureBudget` and `textureLimit`; `mb shot --stats` ends its line with `textures 41.3 of 96 MB (13 of 64)` and `mb measure` adds a `memory max` line. `mb build` estimates the bundle's texture memory if everything were loaded at once and warns, naming the biggest assets, when it's over or near the budget.
- **`mb serve` on a busy port:** when 8765 (or `--port`) is taken, by another preview on the same machine, it uses the next free port and prints the URL it chose, instead of failing.
- **`mb measure`:** runs a game live and reports frame times, GPU time, input latency and a replay check.
  - Startup frames (the first 0.5 s, or `--from SECS`) get a line of their own instead of counting as long frames.
  - `--json` has every frame's interval (`intervals`), also when stepping on a timer (`--hz 120`).
  - Touch latency adds `received→submit`, the page's own part, with a note that the rest of a DevTools touch's latency is Chrome's plumbing.
  - A GPU number you can trust: the scene at the same game times every run (a third, two thirds and the end, or `--gpu-at 5,20`) is drawn over and over in repeats, and reported as `3.08 ± 0.18` ms (95% CI) instead of a second of paced steps after the live run. `--json`'s `gpu` replaces `pacedGpu` and `paced`.
  - The GPU can be timed in several browser sessions (`--gpu-sessions N`): the live run's, then fresh ones. The ± is over the sessions, which differ by more than repeats within one do, so two runs of one build give overlapping intervals. One session by default (the report says its ± is within that session only); `--final` times it in three, for numbers to keep, and `--ab` uses three.
  - Faster while tuning: `--quick` skips the live run and the replay check and times the GPU in one short sample at the `--gpu-at` moments, with what the stepped frames cost and drew (mistwood: 5 s, against 15 s for a default run and 28 s for `--final`).
  - The report is one short block (frames, CPU, GPU, draws, budget, latency, verify); `--verbose` prints every line as before, `--json` everything.
  - `--ab <other build>` compares two builds' GPU time, alternating between them in each session, and reports B − A with a 95% confidence interval over the sessions.
  - `--ab-param shafts=1,0` prices one feature: the same build twice, A with one value of a debug parameter (`sys::param`) and B with the other, alternated like `--ab`. Repeatable.
  - `--ab` with a game directory builds it against the same SDK as A: a copy of an older commit outside the workspace (which names the kit's git repository) gets A's kit patched in, and two builds that would compile against different SDKs are refused, never silently compared.
  - `mb.gpu(n)` in the preview's console times n redraws of the paused frame.
- **`mb shot --smoothness`:** finds frames where the camera jumps, kinks, or behaves differently at 60 and 120 Hz.
  - Each spike lists what the game logged (`sys::log`) within two frames of it.
  - `sys::mark("jump")` marks an impulse the game means: a spike there is reported as `marked: jump` and not counted. `mb.step()` and `mb.stats()` list each frame's marks and log lines.
  - Each spike lists the lines the game logged within two frames of it.
  - `sys::mark("jump")` marks an impulse the game means: a spike there is reported as `marked: jump` and not counted. `mb.step()` and `mb.stats()` list a frame's marks and logs.
- **`--smoothness` watches the field of view:** a zoom that pops (`camera fov`) is a spike like a snap, with the same 60/120 Hz verdicts, and a near or far plane that steps within a frame is listed (`camera clip`). `mb.stats()` and `mb.step()` frames' `camera` gains `fovY`, `near` and `far`.
- **Budget warnings, by node:** a frame over the skill's per-frame budgets (≤ 200 draws, ≤ 150 k triangles, the reflection counted in both; ≤ 16 skinned draws; ≤ 5000 particles) gets a warning under its `mb shot` (with or without `--stats`) and a `budget` line in `mb measure`. Both name the nodes that cost the most triangles (instances × mesh, plus the reflection), say when several of them draw one mesh, and say when an instanced node spans so much of the level that culling can't skip it. `mb.stats()` adds `top` (the costliest nodes of the last frame) and `budgets`; each over-budget frame carries `over` and `top`. The preview's `?stats` readout turns red while over.
- **Debug parameters reach late moments without editing the game:** `mb shot --param zone=3` (and `mb measure`, `mb.restart({ params })`, the preview's `?param=zone=3`) sets a parameter the game reads with `sys::param("zone")` / `sys::param_as::<usize>("zone")` to start where you want to look. Never set on a device or in the feed; recorded in the session's log, so replays and `mb.verify()` match. Natively, `sys::test::set_param`. `examples/mb3d-demo` takes `--param scene=canal|koi`. New host import `mb_param` (always available).
- **`mb shot` shows the game's logs:** after the shots it prints what the game logged (`sys::log`, info and up), each line with its frame and game time; `--logs` adds debug lines, `--logs=warn` keeps warnings and errors, `--logs-match TEXT` filters. `--sheet` also writes `shot-sheet.txt` (each tile's time, frame and file, and the log). What `mb_init` logs is listed with frame 1 (also in `mb.step()` and `mb.stats()`).
- **`mb shot --hz 120`** and `mb.step(n, { dt })` run a game at any frame rate.
- **`mb shot --stats`** prints one compact line per shot; `--stats=json` gives everything.
- **`mb shot` holds the phone upright** in tilt games unless `--tilt` is given.
- **Realistic tilt:** `--tilt 25@3:0.4` (in `mb shot` and `mb measure`) turns the phone there over 0.4 s with a minimum-jerk profile, as a hand does; without `:SECS` it still tilts at once. Scripted tilt, and the preview's mouse tilt, are sampled at 100 Hz like the phone's, not once per pointer event.
- **`mb new` takes hyphenated names** (`koi-gate` → crate `koi_gate`).
- **`mb --version`** names the commit `mb` was built from and the web runtime it embeds: `mb 0.7.0-dev (build b11bbf6, runtime b95b8bb5)`.

### Input and templates
- **`sys::time()` advances in `cargo test`:** natively it's a per-thread clock the test drives (`sys::test::advance(dt)`, `set_time`, `reset`, or `sys::test::update(&mut game, dt)` to run a frame as the host does), so beat grids and `play_at` scheduling can be tested. Before, it stayed at 0.
- **Shared input code:** the templates route raw input to actions through a pure `control.rs` that both the game and its bot use.
- **`input::synth`:** scripts taps, holds and swipes at the touch panel's rate for bots and tests.
  - `synth::flick(from, to, t0, secs, seed)` is a swipe as a thumb makes it: speed in a bell, lifting while still moving, a slightly bowed path, small noise in position and timing.
- **Tuned templates:** both templates' difficulty curves now land inside the skill's round-length targets.
- **Bots with human numbers:** `synth::Skill::{first_timer, casual, good, expert}` are research-based presets of how precise people are with each input (sources in the docs): reaction time, lapses, timing noise, and a Fitts'-law model per input (`touch` in points, `point` for aiming by pointing the phone, `tilt`) that gives a move's time and where it lands, plus tremor and swipes. `skill.aim(..)` swings the phone onto a target as that player would (`synth::aim` is still the ideal hand), `skill.tap(..)` and `skill.flick(..)` are a finger's. Both templates' bots take their reaction, finger speed, aim error and timing from them (presets now first-timer, casual, good), and the bubble template's curve is retuned for fingers as slow as real ones. The skill has a "difficulty sanity check": calibrate the bot against a recorded human run and trust "it's too easy" from the owner over bot numbers.

### Cards and UI
- **Talking portraits and dialogue:** `ui::Portrait` draws a face from a portrait sheet, its mouth following the voice line and blinking on game time. `ui::Dialogue` shows the speaker, typewriter text in step with the voice, and a queue of cutscene lines (tap to finish, tap to advance), or barks during play that never block input and have a cooldown. `ui::VoiceLine` loads a line's `.ogg` and `.lips.txt` and still runs as subtitles without sound. `ui::Ducker` lowers the music 3–6 dB while someone speaks. See docs/UI.md.
- **Voiced lines are never cut off:** a cutscene line's arrow comes up only once its voice has finished, and a tap while the last words are still being spoken moves on when they end (another tap still skips). `.auto_advance(pause)` moves each line on by itself a pause after its voice, so a scene lasts as long as its lines; `dialogue.remaining()` says how long that is, and `dialogue.skip()` skips a line. A cutscene queued during a bark waits for the bark.
- **Bark pools that don't nag:** `dialogue.barks` picks barks from pools tagged by situation (`taunt`, `part_broken`, `low_health`, `defeat`…) when the game calls `trigger(tag)`: a shuffle bag per pool, no line again within the last 3 barks, a cooldown per pool (8 s), at most 4 barks a minute per character, and a late event's bark dropped after 1 s. `trigger_priority` barks wait their turn instead. Only one voice at a time: a bark never starts over another bark or a cutscene line, and barks are 5 s apart (was 3 s; a bark still being spoken is no longer replaced). Idle chatter is off unless `barks.idle(tag, every)`. `barks.load_script(include_str!("../art/lines.csv"))` loads an `mb voice script` file, pools from its new optional `tag` column or the line ids. See docs/UI.md.
- **A card between fights:** `ui::StatsCard` shows a title ("FIGHT 2 CLEAR"), stat rows rolling up one by one (`.percent`, `.int`, `.time`; `.bad()` for costs), a rank letter stamped on (`.rank`, `StatsCard::rank_for`), the next opponent sliding in with their portrait (`.next`, `draw_with`), and an iris, slide or shutter wipe into the next scene (`StatsEvent::Covered` says when to swap). Game time only, a tap reveals everything and the next leaves, themed, inside the card's safe area. The gallery's STATS page (`--param page=11`) shows it. See docs/UI.md.
- **Your own cards:** `ResultsCard` exposes its state (shown score, new-best moment, retry rule), and `.state_only()` lets a game draw its own card. `TitleCard::age()` is new.
- **`Text::vcenter()`** centers vertically without touching the horizontal alignment.
- **`replay_banner = "top"`** in the manifest moves a replay card's banner to the top, for runners and racers whose player sits low on the screen.
- **Accented text draws, in every font:** Bolt Brawl's "Touché" came out "Touch?" because `mb font add` (and the fonts `mb new` bakes for an identity) baked ASCII only. Fonts now bake Latin-1 by default (`--chars latin1`: é ñ ü ç ß, ¿ ¡ « » £ €, curly quotes and dashes), and `caps` covers Latin-1 too, drawing é as É. It about doubles a font: EB Garamond 40 → 81 KB (36 → 63 KB in the compressed bundle), Outfit 800 51 → 107 KB (40 → 74 KB). `--chars ascii` still bakes the small set. Fonts already in a game's fonts.toml keep their `chars` until you change it; `mb build` says when the text needs more. The built-in pixel font has Latin-1 now too, plus ‘ ’ “ ” – — €: accented lowercase uses the rows above the x-height, accented capitals reach two pixels above the cap height. Inter already had it. The host's fonts grow 2.3 KB (1.1 KB gzipped). The UI gallery's TEXT page shows both.

### Sound
- **Voice acting: `mb voice`.**
  - `mb voice design <game> <who> "<description>"` designs an original voice (ElevenLabs Voice Design) and writes three previews; `mb voice pick` keeps one. The cast is in `art/voices.toml`. Clones, impressions and "sounds like <a name>" are refused.
  - `mb voice say <game> <who> <line> "<text>" --emotion smug` speaks a line with `eleven_v4` (audio tags act the emotion) into `assets/vo/<who>/<line>.ogg`, made for a phone: mono, −16 dBFS, trimmed, a high-pass and a presence boost, the phone-loss check. `--variants N` and `mb voice keep` to choose a take.
  - Each line gets a `.lips.txt`: the mouth at 30 Hz (closed to wide, shut on m/b/p) and when each character of the subtitle is spoken, from the provider's timings.
  - `mb voice script <game> lines.csv` speaks a whole script with the cost first, only the lines missing or changed; `--provider mock` makes free stand-ins with real timings.
  - Parallel runs are safe: every `mb` run on a machine queues for the ElevenLabs account's 3 concurrent requests (lock files; `MB_ELEVENLABS_CONCURRENCY` for another plan), a second run of the same line is refused instead of overwriting the first, and a line's files are written whole (renamed into place).
  - `mb voice design` prints each preview's length, median pitch (Hz), loudness and phone loss, and saves them with the preview in `art/voices.toml`, so the cast can be picked (and kept apart) by numbers too.
  - `--speed` works on every model: Flash and the other v2 models take it from the API (as a plain two-decimal number, which Flash needs); Eleven v3 and v4 ignore the setting, so mb time-stretches their lines after, pitch kept, and says so.
- **Voice slots:** an ElevenLabs account holds a fixed number of custom voices (10 on Starter) and every kept voice takes one for good.
  - `mb voice slots` shows how many are used, which voice each game under the repository uses, which voices no game uses, and temporary ones an interrupted batch left behind.
  - `mb voice design` and `mb voice pick` check for a free slot first: a full account is refused before anything is spent, with what to do (`--ignore-slots` designs anyway; previews don't take a slot).
  - `mb voice release <game> <who>|--all [--delete --yes]` frees a voice's slot and keeps the voice in the game as audio: the picked preview and its first lines, in `art/voice_ref/<who>/` (commit it). Lines for a released character are spoken with a recreation (ElevenLabs Instant Voice Cloning from that audio only) made once per batch and deleted after it; `--keep` keeps it for later batches. A recreation is very close to the designed voice, not identical: speak a character's lines in one batch.
  - `mb voice gc` lists the account's voices no game here speaks with (released ones, temporary ones left by an interrupted batch, unused ones) and deletes exactly that list only with `--yes` (`--except` keeps one). It refuses when it finds no game's `art/voices.toml`, and leaves voices mb made for games it can't see (another checkout or branch) unless `--other-games`.
  - `mb voice premades [female british …]` lists ElevenLabs' premade voices with their gender, age and accent, and `mb voice cast <game> <who> --premade <name>` casts one (no slot needed), with the character's default `--delivery`, `--style`, `--speed` and `--model`.
  - A key missing a permission gets a clear error naming it (`voices_read`, `voices_write`, `user_read`, `text_to_speech`) and where to turn it on. A full account's refusal to keep or recreate a voice says `no free voice slot: run mb voice slots`, with the ways out; a month's voice adds and edits used up says so.
- **Music that isn't the same 8 bars:** `mb music --zones "lantern town: …; bamboo grove: …"`, `--sections a,b,bridge` or `--variations 4`.
  - One piece in sections, asked for in one Lyria 3.5 request with a timestamped line per section, so it keeps one key, tempo and bar grid.
  - The grid is found once over the whole take, its 1 from harmonic changes as well as low onsets; no section keeps a guess of its own. `--downbeat N` moves it N beats, from the take you have.
  - **A music graph** (the default): the take itself (`<name>_take`, or `_take_base` + `_take_hi`) and `<name>.music.json`, how a game moves through it the way shipped games do adaptive music:
    - Each section is a segment: its body between an entry cue and an exit cue on the piece's bars, with the take's own pickup before it (pre-entry) and tail after it (post-exit). Entries are moved up to two bars onto where the music changes.
    - Exit cues at the end of each 4-bar phrase; a transition table says for each pair of zones whether the take runs from one into the other, where a seamless link goes from one into the other, and what covers a change without one (the zone's stinger, or the swell).
    - Jump links, as in the Infinite Jukebox: the take is analysed beat by beat (harmony, level and timbre per band, rhythm), and bar lines whose bar before and bar after sound alike are joined, with scores. Each zone's threshold starts at 0.85 and comes down to 0.7 until it has enough links. At a zone's end, a link back, or a restart from its entry under the swell.
    - The log prints each section's links, each pair of zones and each zone's way back.
  - `--loops` also cuts a loop per section that repeats on its own, as before the graph:
    - Each section's tempo and key are measured. Small slips are corrected: up to 5 % of tempo (time-stretched, pitch kept) and one semitone of key. Anything further is reported, and mb asks once more (`--retries`).
    - Every loop is exactly the same length and starts on the piece's one bar grid, its 1 10 ms in, so a switch on any bar keeps the bar.
    - Loops that join: each section's loop is the window of whole bars whose end runs into its start most like the music did (`loop_match` in the log and sidecar), a 4-bar phrase written twice when no 8-bar loop joins. The bar that followed it is cross-faded into its start (equal power, `--loop-xfade` bars, default 1), so the file repeats seamlessly at its exact length; single loops get the same crossfade.
    - `--fix-loops`: a section that still joins under 0.85 gets one Lyria 3 Clip of its own (~$0.04), fitted to the piece's tempo and key within the same limits and used only if it joins better. Off unless asked; `--reprocess` reuses the clips.
  - `--audition`: every jump, restart and change between zones the Director makes in one file, spliced as it splices them, a soft click before each (loops: every join and switch).
  - A stinger into each zone (`--zones`, `--sections`): a two-beat riser that lands on the chord the zone opens with in the take. Every piece in sections gets `_swell`, a one-beat swell for changes without a stinger.
  - Longer single loops: `--bars 32` uses Lyria 3.5 when Lyria 3 Clip's 30 s is too short.
- **`maimbrain::music::Director`:** plays it in a game.
  - A music graph (`Director::from_graph`, `music::Graph`): the take straight through, with a jump link now and then (seeded, so replays hear the same music; never a jump back of under 8 bars), so a zone plays on without a loop point.
  - Changes of zone (`Change::Auto`) at the next good point: a seamless link into the zone, the take running into it, or the next exit cue under the zone's stinger, at most a bar past the first bar line it could use. `Change::NextBar` doesn't wait.
  - Every join overlaps two voices: the new one's pre-entry fades in before the bar line, the old one's post-exit dies away after it. The base and high layers jump together, and the tension kit follows the take's phrase.
  - Loops (`Director::new`, `mb music --loops`): switches on the bar under the new loop's stinger or the piece's swell, whose hit lands on the bar the new loop starts, or a crossfade; rotates a zone's variations every N bars.
  - Drives the high layer from intensity and the tension kit from one `danger` value: the tension layer, the clock, a heartbeat on the beat, the low-pass, a sting on a near-catch and relief on the escape.
  - `Director::stop(now, fade)` lets every voice it started die away (the take, loops, tension, clock, heartbeat, stingers) and opens the music bus; `restart(zone, now)` starts again, and a second `start` stops the first set first, so there are never two sets of voices.
  - Tension levels that fit the take: `mb music` measures the take on a phone (mix_check's K-weighting and 300 Hz high-pass) and writes the tension kit's level into the music graph (`levels.tension_gain`, ×1–2.86); `Director::from_graph` plays at it (`Levels::for_graph`), so a loud Lyria take still lifts a clear +3 dB on a phone at full danger. Older graphs keep the defaults. A kit test renders the Director over a real take excerpt the way mix_check does, so the defaults can't drift from the mix rules.
  - Its `Log` output writes a `mix_check.py` log for tests. `sdk/maimbrain/examples/chase_music.rs` is a chase through three zones. A custom `music::Out` adds `play_from` and `stop_at`.
  - **`mix_check.py`** reads `from=`, `fade=` and `id=` on a log line: a voice started part-way into its sound, faded in, released over a fade, two voices of one sound told apart. A music graph's take counts as music.
- **Sounds from any point, and voices that die away on time** (SPEC §5.6):
  - `Sound::play_from` and `play_looped_from` (`mb_play_from`) start a sound part-way through, heard at a game time, rising from silence over a fade you give. Started late, they come in further along, on the grid.
  - `Voice::stop_at` (`mb_voice_stop_at`) lets a voice die away from a game time, like a note let go: 60 dB over the fade, then it stops.
  - Together they let a player move through one long take bar by bar, the new voice coming in under the old one's tail. Existing calls work as before.
- **New layers for a track you have, for free:** `mb music <game> <name> --reprocess --layers base,hi,tension` or `mb regen <file> --add-layers tension`. Both work from the take in `art/raw/`, with no new take and no sidecar editing.
- **Tension you can hear on a phone:** the skill's levels go up: the tension layer to vol 1.4 at full danger, the clock to 1.0, the heartbeat to 0.15–0.3, the sting to 0.7. That lifts the music 3–5 dB on a phone at full danger; the old levels gave 2–2.6 dB, and a heartbeat at 0.1 failed mix_check. The kit's sting is brightened for a phone speaker.
- **`mix_check.py` treats tension as music meant to rise:**
  - The music's typical level comes from the track's own layers.
  - It reports how much the tension lifts the music on a phone. It flags under +3 dB at its peak, and more than +1 dB at its quietest (dread that never lets go).
  - A heartbeat over the tension isn't capped like a frequent effect. Its loudest quarter of plays must be heard on a phone.
- **Music that builds dread as a threat closes in:** `mb music --layers base,hi,tension`.
  - Adds a tension layer (drone, pulse, heartbeat, string tremolo, risers) and a ticking clock. Both are exactly the loop's length, on its beat and in its measured key.
  - Adds a heartbeat and a sting in the same key, as one-shots.
  - They're synthesized to fit the track: Lyria can't make a stem that lines up with another take.
  - The skill's "Music that follows the game" has the recipe. Map the chaser's distance to the tension, narrow the music with a low-pass, quicken the heartbeat on the beat, play the sting on a near-catch and release on the escape.
- **`mb music` reports the track's key** (measured the way `mix_check.py` does) in its log and sidecar.
- **Phone checks when a sound is made:**
  - `mb sfx` and `mb music` say how much a phone speaker loses of each file. Past 10 dB, `mb sfx` warns.
  - `--brighten` (or `mb regen <file> --reprocess --brighten`, free) adds the bass's harmonics a phone can play. A deep thud still sounds deep.
  - `check_audio.py` prints the same number and flags bass-heavy effects.
- **Edits after generation are recorded:** `mb art edited <file> --tool <script> -- <args>` notes the edit in the sidecar. `mb art check` then says how the file was edited, instead of only that it changed.
- **`mix_check.py` measures layered music:** it prints each music layer's level and takes the key from the track's own layers, not from the tension layers.
- **`mix_check.py` listens through a phone speaker.**
  - Pickups and other frequent sounds must be heard over the music.
  - Fatigue is capped by energy over time, not by keeping peaks under the music.
  - New flags: too quiet to hear on a phone, and masked by the music.
- **Pickup design guidance:** short, bright, layered, a pitch ladder, a sweetener on milestones.
- **Kit levels:** the `runner` and `racer` kits' coins are louder.
