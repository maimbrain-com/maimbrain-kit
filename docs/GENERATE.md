# Generated art, sound and 3D models (`mb art`, `mb music`, `mb sfx`, `mb model`)

`mb` can ask an image or audio model for a game's assets and turn what comes back into files that drop straight into `assets/`: exact sizes, transparent sprites, pixel grids, the game's palette, seamless tiles and layers, PNGs within budget; music cut to a seamless whole-bar loop, effects trimmed, everything mono Ogg Vorbis at the platform's loudness (SPEC §5.6); 3D models as `.glb` files within a triangle budget, in meters, facing the right way, with their animation clips (SPEC §5.4). Every asset gets a provenance sidecar, and a per-game style guide keeps one game's art consistent.

You can still draw art and synthesize sound with scripts (the maimbrain-game skill's `pixel.py` and `sfx.py`). The skill explains when to use which.

## Quick start

```sh
mb keys set gemini                     # paste a Gemini API key (or export GEMINI_API_KEY)
mb art init games/moth                 # games/moth/art/style.toml: style words, palette, references
mb art sprite games/moth hero "a moth with stained-glass wings, wings spread"
mb music games/moth music "dreamy harp and glass chimes over a soft pulse" --bars 8
mb music games/moth run "a tense taiko chase" --zones "lantern town: warm lanterns; bamboo grove: hollow wood" --bpm 120   # one take, a zone per place, and its music graph
mb sfx games/moth chime "a bright glass chime, short"      # needs an ElevenLabs key
mb model games/moth lantern "a paper lantern painted with moths" --glow 2   # needs a Tripo key (or Maimbrain's 3D access); see 3D models
mb art check games/moth                # budgets, and what made each asset
```

If the Maimbrain team has given your account access to **Maimbrain's keys**, you don't need a key of your own: sign in with `mb login` and the same commands work (see [Maimbrain's keys](#maimbrains-keys)).

No key yet? Add `--provider mock` to any command: the offline mock provider makes deterministic placeholder art and sound, so the whole pipeline can be tried (and is tested) without a network. `mb art check` flags mock assets so they don't ship by accident. `--dry-run` prints the exact prompt and the estimated cost without calling anything, key or not.

## Commands

All image commands take the game directory first, then the asset name (output `assets/<name>.png`), then a plain description of the subject.

|---|---|---|

| `mb art layers <game> <name> "<prompt>" --count N [--screens 2]` | parallax layers `<name>_0…` far to near; layer 0 opaque by design (the sky and the farthest shapes; or use a painted `mb art sky` and only the nearer layers), the rest transparent, keyed from their sky (the top rows and the key color the prompt asked for, not the border, which is mostly scenery) and cropped with their ground kept (the bottom at the lowest solid row, the last rows filled solid, since a backdrop repeats its bottom row downward); all wrap horizontally; later layers get layer 0 as a palette and light reference (not a subject), and the sidecar's `result.layer` says which is which | 2 screens wide |

Shared image options: `--size WxH` (output pixels; the canvas is 2 px per logical unit, so a 60-unit-wide sprite is 120 px), `--grid WxH` (pixel art: the art-pixel grid; output = grid × the style's `pixel_scale`), `--ref <photo>` (repeatable: redraw this photo's or sketch's subject in the game's style), `--seed N`, `--variants N` (N candidates in `art/variants/` plus a contact sheet), `--provider` (`maimbrain` forces Maimbrain's keys), `--own-keys` (never Maimbrain's keys), `--model`, `--res 0.5K|1K|2K|4K`, `--quality low|medium|high` (OpenAI), `--anchor center|bottom`, `--margin PX`, `--colors N`, `--max-kb N` (quantize until the PNG fits), `--keep-background`, `--tolerance` (keying), `--out PATH`, `--no-style`, `--allow-name WORD`, `--max-cost USD`, `--dry-run`.

Music options: `--bars N` (past ~26 s of music, gemini uses `lyria-3.5`: Lyria 3 Clip is always 30 s), `--bpm B` (asked for, and used instead of detection), `--seconds S` (free-length loop for pieces without a steady beat), `--layers` (also writes `<name>_base.ogg` and `<name>_hi.ogg`; `--layers base,hi,tension` adds the [tension kit](#tension-layers), `--layers tension` keeps the full mix and adds it), `--key "A minor"` (the key for the tension kit instead of the measured one), `--split-hz 700`, `--once` (a jingle or sting, not a loop), `--negative` (Vertex lyria-002), `--rate`, `--quality`. [Music in sections](#music-in-sections): `--variations N`, `--sections a,b,bridge`, `--zones "name: description; …"` (or `@file`), `--retries N` (default 1), `--loop-xfade BARS` (the crossfade baked into each loop, default 1), `--loops` (also a self-looping loop per section, for `Director::new`; the music graph is written either way), `--fix-loops [BELOW]` (with `--loops`: a Lyria 3 Clip for each loop that joins under 0.85; costs money), `--downbeat N` (move the piece's 1 by N beats, from the take you have), `--audition [PATH]` (the graph's jumps and every change between zones in one file). `--reprocess` redoes the post-processing from the take you have, with the options given now ([new layers for a take you have](#new-layers-for-a-take-you-have)). Effects: `--seconds`, `--loop`, `--brighten` (when a phone speaker would lose more than 10 dB of it, add its bass's harmonics; below).

`mb regen <asset>` options: `--seed N` or `--new-seed`, `--prompt "…"`, `--provider`/`--model`, `--variants N`, `--reprocess` (redo only the post-processing from the saved raw output: free and offline; use it after changing the palette, colors, size rules or keying), `--tolerance T` and `--key '#rrggbb'` (images: keying tolerance, and the background color to key out; by default the one the asset was generated with, recorded as the recipe's `key`), `--brighten`/`--no-brighten` (effects), `--dry-run`, `--max-cost`.

Sources: [Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing), [Vertex AI pricing](https://cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/pricing), [OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing), [ElevenLabs API pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing/api). `mb models` prints the table mb uses. Every command prints its estimated cost before calling (OpenAI responses report their token usage, and mb prints the actual cost), refuses when one command would cost more than **$1.00** (`--max-cost`, or `MB_MAX_COST`), and records the estimate in the sidecar; `mb art check` adds up what a game's assets cost.

Why these defaults: Nano Banana 2.1 is Google's current image workhorse and costs the same at 1K as the Lite model, while following style references and multi-image instructions much better; Pro is 4× the price for briefs that need it. Lyria 3 Clip always makes 30 s, which holds an 8-bar loop at about 80 BPM or faster (4 bars below that), for $0.04. Google's image and music APIs embed a SynthID watermark; the sidecar notes it. Gemini 2.5 Flash Image (the original "Nano Banana") was shut down on 2 October 2026 and isn't offered.

Google has no sound-effect model in its APIs, so `mb sfx` uses ElevenLabs, whose free plan includes a few effect generations a month (check its license terms: the free plan isn't licensed for commercial use). Without an ElevenLabs key, make effects procedurally with the skill's `scripts/sfx.py` (8-bit voices, sweeps, noise, a step sequencer): it already meets the same loudness and encoding rules, and costs nothing.

### What to set up

- **Gemini API (recommended; images and music):** sign in at [Google AI Studio](https://aistudio.google.com/apikey), create an API key, and **enable billing on its Google Cloud project** (AI Studio → Billing): the image and music models have no free tier on the API (you can try them for free in the AI Studio web UI, not through the API). New accounts may be asked to prepay $10. Then `mb keys set gemini` or `export GEMINI_API_KEY=…`.
- **Vertex AI (alternative; where Google Cloud free-trial credits apply):** a Google Cloud project with the Vertex AI API enabled and the [gcloud CLI](https://cloud.google.com/sdk/docs/install) signed in (`gcloud auth login`). `mb keys set vertex` takes the **project id**; mb gets a short-lived token from `gcloud auth print-access-token` for each call (or set `VERTEX_ACCESS_TOKEN`). `GOOGLE_CLOUD_LOCATION` defaults to `global` (lyria-002 uses `us-central1`). New Google Cloud customers get $300 in credits, which as of 2026 apply to Vertex AI but not to Gemini API keys made in AI Studio.
- **ElevenLabs (sound effects; free plan available):** create an account, then an API key at [elevenlabs.io/app/settings/api-keys](https://elevenlabs.io/app/settings/api-keys) with access to Sound Effects (and Music, for music). `mb keys set elevenlabs`.
- **OpenAI (optional, for native transparency):** an API key at [platform.openai.com/api-keys](https://platform.openai.com/api-keys); GPT Image models may require [organization verification](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10910291-api-organization-verification). `mb keys set openai`.

Keys are read from the environment first, then from `~/.config/maimbrain/keys.json` (`%APPDATA%\maimbrain\keys.json` on Windows; mode 0600), next to `mb login`'s credentials. Nothing is written into a game or repository. `mb keys list` shows which are set (masked) and where each comes from; `mb doctor` reports them too.

### Maimbrain's keys

The Maimbrain team can let individual creators generate on **Maimbrain's** Gemini and ElevenLabs keys instead of their own, and, with a second grant, 3D models on its Tripo key. Access is granted per account by an admin (there's no way to request it from `mb`: ask the team), and comes with a monthly budget, $10 unless the team sets another, that resets on the 1st (UTC) and covers art, sound and 3D together.

Which keys a command uses:

1. **Your own key** for the chosen provider, if you have one (environment or `mb keys`). You pay; nothing changes.
2. Otherwise, if you're signed in (`mb login`) and the server says your account has access, **Maimbrain's keys**: mb sends the provider request to the server, which calls the provider and returns the raw image or sound (for 3D, mb's Tripo client talks to the server, which passes each task to Tripo; below). Everything else (keying, fitting, loops, loudness, the sidecar) still happens on your machine. The command prints `keys: Maimbrain's (@you) · $1.20 of $10.00 used this month` before the call and the new total after it.
3. Otherwise the usual "set a key" message, which also says how to get access.

Without a provider named, mb picks the first provider you have a key for (images: gemini, vertex, openai; music: gemini, vertex, elevenlabs; effects: elevenlabs; 3D models: tripo, meshy), then the first of those Maimbrain's keys cover.

| To | Do |
|---|---|
| never use Maimbrain's keys | `--own-keys`, or `MB_KEYS_SOURCE=own` |
| always use Maimbrain's keys, even with a key of your own | `--provider maimbrain` (the default model for the kind), or `MB_KEYS_SOURCE=maimbrain` with `--provider gemini`/`elevenlabs`/`tripo` to pick one |
| see your access and this month's spend | `mb models` (models marked `M` are covered) or `mb doctor` |

What they cover: `gemini` images (Nano Banana 2.1, 2 Lite, 2 and Pro, at 0.5K–2K; not 4K) and music (Lyria 3 Clip, Lyria 3.5), `elevenlabs` sound effects and music, and, for accounts with 3D access, `tripo` models (P1 and H3.1, rigs and clips; [below](#3d-models-on-maimbrains-key)). Not `vertex`, `openai`, `meshy` or voices: use your own key for those. Each call is charged against your budget at the list price above (the server's estimate, recorded in the sidecar as `cost_usd`, with `"via": "maimbrain"`); failed calls cost nothing. mb refuses up front when its estimate is more than what's left; the server refuses (HTTP 402) when the budget is spent, and allows two calls at a time and a few dozen every ten minutes.

The server runs the same originality check as mb on every prompt, and `--allow-name` isn't honored on Maimbrain's keys: if you own the rights to a flagged name, use your own key. The server keeps a record of each call (who, which model, the estimated cost, and a hash of the prompt, not the prompt itself).

### Network blips and busy providers

Every provider call (`mb art`, `music`, `sfx`, `voice`, `model`, and Maimbrain's keys) goes through one HTTP transport that tries a request up to 4 times, waiting 1, 2 and 4 s (or the provider's `Retry-After`, up to 30 s) and saying so (`generativelanguage.googleapis.com: HTTP 503; trying again in 1 s (2/4)`). It never pays twice: a generation (a POST) is sent again only when the provider can't have started it, because the connection failed before the whole request went out (a reset or refused connection, a DNS failure; mb counts the request body as it's sent) or the provider answered 429 or 503, which refuse a request without running it. A connection that drops after the request went out isn't retried, since the provider may have run and billed it; the error says so, and running the command again retries it. Polling and downloads (GETs) are safe to repeat, so they also retry a dropped connection and a 500, 502 or 504. `MB_HTTP_TRIES` sets the number of tries (1 turns retrying off).

### Adding a provider

`crates/mb-cli/src/generate/provider.rs` defines the `Provider` trait (`image`, `audio`, `model`), the request types, the model catalog with prices and a `Transport` trait for HTTP. A provider is one file that builds a request body, posts it through the transport and decodes the response (`google.rs`, `openai.rs`, `elevenlabs.rs`); a 3D provider also polls its tasks with `Poll` and downloads results with `download` (`tripo.rs`, `meshy.rs`). Its tests replace the transport with canned responses, so they check request bodies and response parsing with no network.

`maimbrain.rs` is the provider that sends the same request to the Maimbrain server instead (and decides which keys a call uses). To offer a provider or model on Maimbrain's keys too, add it to `SERVER_PROVIDERS` there and to the server's `Generation::Catalog` and `Generation::Client` (`server/app/services/generation/`, docs/SERVER.md "Generation"); keep the two price tables in step. Tripo is the exception: its client (`Tripo::on_maimbrain`) talks to the server's pass-through of Tripo's own paths (`Generation::Tripo`, which allows only the paths and fields mb sends), so a new Tripo call needs a route and a `Generation::TripoRequest` step there.

## 3D models (`mb model`)

`mb model` asks a text-to-3D service for a model and turns what comes back into a `.glb` that loads in mb3d as is (SPEC §5.4): textured with PBR maps, within a triangle budget, in meters, facing the way games expect, with its clips named for `Animator`, checked the way the validator and mb3d will check it. Use it for organic, detailed things that look poor built from primitives: creatures, characters, props with sculpted shape and painted texture. Keep building in code what the rules depend on (assets.md says when).

```sh
mb keys set tripo                       # a Tripo API key (https://platform.tripo3d.ai), credits bought
mb model games/canal koi "a stylized koi carp, orange and white, glowing fins" --animate swim --size 0.7 --glow 2.5
mb model games/run hero --prompt "a fox adventurer in a green cloak" --animate idle,run --size 1.2
mb model games/run crate "a weathered wooden crate with iron corners" --polys 400 --size 0.8
mb model games/run lamp "a paper lantern" --ref art/ref/lantern-sketch.png     # from a drawing or render
mb model games/duel gun "a twin-barrel shoulder cannon, gunmetal with orange stripes" --kind part --size 1.4
```

| Option | Does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `"<prompt>"` or `--prompt` | what it is: subject, shape, colors, materials | |
| `--ref IMAGE` | make it from an image (a drawing, a render, a photo of something you own) | |
| `--kind character\|part\|prop\|vehicle` | what it is, which decides the style words: a character gets the style guide's `[3d]` words, `style` and `keywords`; a part (a gun, a blade, an armor plate), a prop or a vehicle is one hard-surface object and gets `[3d_object]`'s words and the palette instead, with "character, robot, face, arms, legs…" in the negative prompt (below). A part is centered on the origin (it hangs on a socket) | guessed from the prompt's first word that names one ("a beam saber for a giant robot" is a part); rigged or animated: character |
| `--polys N` | triangle budget: asked of the provider, then enforced by simplifying | 8000 |
| `--rig`, `--rig-type T` | add a skeleton; tripo's types: biped, quadruped, hexapod, octopod, avian, serpentine, aquatic (the provider guesses without `--rig-type`). With no prompt, on a model made earlier: rig it from its recorded task (below) | |
| `--rig-task ID` | on a model made earlier: adopt this rig task instead of rigging again; `--animate` adds clips to it | |
| `--animate a,b` | clips: `walk`, `run`, `idle`, `swim`, `jump`… or a provider's own id (tripo `preset:biped:wave_goodbye_01`, a Meshy action id); implies `--rig`; each becomes a clip named `Walk`, `Run`… On Tripo a name that isn't one of its presets (`punch`, `defeat_01`) is refused before anything is spent, with what to ask for instead; `mb model presets` lists them | |
| `--clip E,…` | edit the clips: `Idle=0..4` keeps 0–4 s of Idle, `Strike=Slash@1.8..3.4` cuts a new clip from part of Slash (Slash goes unless an edit keeps it, `Slash=0.6..3.4`), `Swing=Slash` renames, `Chop=drop` drops; open ends work (`2..`). Clip names match the way `--animate` names them (`hit_to_body_01` is `HitToBody01`). In the recipe: with `--reprocess`, an edit replaces the recorded one for the same clip and `--clip none` clears them | |
| `--size M` | the longest side, meters | 1 |
| `--origin ground\|center` | stands on y = 0, or centered on the origin | center for swimmers (a swim clip or a fish prompt) and parts, else ground |
| `--facing -z\|+z` | which way it faces: −z is where the camera looks and runners run (no turn needed in a runner); +z is glTF's convention (`make_rigged_glb.py`'s fox) | −z |
| `--turn DEG`, `--lengthwise` | fix a model that came back sideways: turn it about +y, or turn its longest horizontal side onto z (fish, cars, boats) | |
| `--orient "FEATURE=AXIS [FEATURE=AXIS]"` | turn a part into a stated convention from its shape (below): `tip=+y` (a blade or barrel's slim end up, its flat side to +z), `tip=+z up=+y` (a gun's muzzle forward, its top up), `face=+z` (a plate or shield's outer face to +z, its top up). | `--texture-size PX` | the largest texture side: 1024 for a hero or anything seen close (its texture is its detail), 512 for mid-size things, 256 for small props or crowds; `--reprocess --texture-size N` changes it later, free and offline | 1024 |
| `--maps-size PX` | the largest side of the normal and metal-rough maps, separately from the base color: under paint they barely show, and a half-size normal map reads the same at phone distance | half `--texture-size`, at least 64 |
| `--metal K`, `--min-roughness R` | matte a chrome-like model: metalness × K (0.15 keeps 15 %), roughness at least R (baked into the metal-rough map). | `--texture-format auto\|jpeg\|png` | auto is JPEG unless a texture's alpha is used | auto |
| `--no-compress` | write float geometry instead of quantized (below); `--reprocess --compress` turns it back on | quantized |
| `--glow S`, `--glow-above V` | an emissive map from the texture's bright parts (texel value above V, 0 = everything) at strength S; 2–4 blooms. On a white or yellow model that lights all of it: use `--glow-color` | |
| `--glow-color '#rrggbb[,…]'`, `--glow-tolerance D` | light only the texels near these colors (a gem, a reactor core, coolant stripes, a muzzle): Oklab distance with lightness counted half, so the color's shaded texels match too, under D; the mask grows a texel and is softened, and the glow is the texel's own color a little brighter. Strength from `--glow` (else 3). The run prints the share of the texture lit and warns at none or over 40 %. `--reprocess --no-glow` drops a recorded glow | 0.15 |
| `--negative "…"` | what to keep out (tripo; the style's `avoid` is added) | |
| `--rigid [BONES]` | hard-surface weights: each vertex follows only its strongest bone, so armor plates move as plates instead of bending like skin. `all` (the default), groups (`arms`, `legs`, `spine`, `head`) or joint names; `--rigid off` clears it. Needs a biped's named rig | |
| `--pin REGION=BONE` | re-weight what hangs off a limb (below) to the body: `cape=spine` (behind the body; the spine chain by height, so the top follows the upper back and the hem the hips), `skirt=hips` (below the hips at the front and sides), `hanging=…` (both), `auto` (cape=spine, skirt=hips); a bone is `spine`, `chest`, `hips`, `waist` or a joint name; `--pin none` clears them. Needs a biped's named rig | |
| `--spine [auto\|head-to-tail\|wings]`, `--spine-joints N` | code-driven bones instead of the provider's rig: a chain of joints along the body (`spine_0` at the head … the tail), smooth weights, and for birds two-bone wings (`wing_l_0/1`, `wing_r_0/1` under a `wings` node); the game bends it with `Skeleton::spine` + `Swim`/`Wave`/`Flight` (below). Replaces the provider's rig and clips | off; 8 joints |
| `--import GLB` | process a `.glb` you already have, free and offline (one mb made earlier faces −z; another tool's needs `--turn 180`): placement, budget, textures, `--spine` | |
| `--variants N`, `--pick N` | N candidates in `art/variants/` with a comparison sheet; promote one | 1 |
| `--seed`, `--provider`, `--model`, `--own-keys`, `--out`, `--no-style`, `--allow-name`, `--max-cost`, `--dry-run` | as for the other commands | |

The prompt is built like an image prompt: the subject, `art/style.toml`'s `[3d] prompt`, `style`, `keywords` and palette, then "a single game-ready 3D model with clean, readable shapes that read well small on a phone; the whole object, no base, ground or background", a neutral pose for rigged characters, and "an original design". `--dry-run` shows it with the price.

**Parts, props and vehicles (`--kind`).** A game's style words are written for its characters: "anime mecha", "visible mechanical joints", "real-robot". Sent with a gun or an armor plate, they turn it into a little robot (Bolt Brawl's shoulder guns came back with heads and legs, and only came out right with `--no-style`). So a `--kind part|prop|vehicle` model gets the subject, the style guide's `[3d_object] prompt` (write the look there without naming characters: "crisp panel lines, hand-painted texture, worn paint"), the palette, and "one single solid hard-surface object shown on its own … all in one piece", with "character, robot, figure, face, eyes, head, arms, legs, hands, mechanical joints" in the negative prompt (Tripo; positive "no arms" would ask for arms). Without `--kind`, mb guesses from the prompt (the first word that names a kind wins: "a beam saber for a giant robot" is a part, "a robot holding a saber" a character) and says what it picked; anything rigged or animated is a character. The kind is in the recipe, so `mb regen` keeps it.

**Rigging a model made earlier.** `mb model <game> <name> --rig [--rig-type biped] [--animate idle,slash]` with no prompt works on `assets/<name>.glb` as it is: Tripo rigs it from the generation task its sidecar recorded (`result.tasks`, no new mesh, $0.25), or from an upload of its raw `.glb` when there is none (a model imported or made elsewhere), and retargets the clips. The rig task goes into `result.tasks` and `result.rig_task`, so a later `mb model <game> <name> --animate hit_to_body_01` adds clips to that rig ($0.10 each) without rigging again, and a clip it already has isn't made again. The rigged download replaces the raw model (the unrigged one is kept as `art/raw/<name>-<seed>.unrigged.glb`), the recipe gets `rig`, `rig_type` and the clips, and the model is reprocessed with its whole recipe (any `--reprocess` options given apply too), so a later `--reprocess` keeps the rig and the clips. `--rig-task <id>` adopts a rig made outside mb (or before mb recorded them) instead of paying for another. `--dry-run` prints what it would do and cost.

**Armor that doesn't stretch (`--rigid`, `--pin`).** Auto-riggers weight a mesh like skin: each vertex blends a few bones, so armor bends at the joints like rubber, and anything long that hangs (a cape, a skirt, a binder) is weighted to whatever limb is nearest and stretches when that limb swings. Bolt Brawl's SWORDMASTER came back from Tripo with its heel-length cape weighted to the calves, thighs and forearms. `mb model <game> <name> --reprocess --rigid --pin cape=spine` fixes both, free and offline: `--rigid` gives each vertex its single strongest bone; `--pin` finds the vertices that hang off a limb (led by an arm or leg bone but farther from its bones than 1.8× the limb's own thickness, fully moved past 2.6×, a band between so there's no crease), behind the body (`cape`) or below the hips (`skirt`), and moves them to the body. `spine` means the chain from the hips to the top spine bone, by each vertex's height: pinned to the upper back alone, a heel-length cape swings out like a board whenever the chest leans. The run measures the pinned part over the clips and says how much of it stretched or squashed past 25 % before and after (the SWORDMASTER's cape: 65 % → 5 %; `result.weights`), and writes `art/preview/<name>.weights.png`: each clip's frames before, then after (the rows with a green bar), from behind and to the side. Both are in the recipe, so `--reprocess` keeps them. A cape on the hips follows the hips: it won't flutter (there's no cloth), but it won't stretch either.

**Tripo's clips (`mb model presets`).** Bipeds have 101 presets (`preset:biped:<name>`: `idle`, `walk`, `run`, `slash`, `chop`, `shoot`, `hurt`, `hit_to_body_01`, `box_01`–`03`, `front_kick_01`, `defeat_02`, `jump_down`…); the other rig types one each. `mb model presets` prints them all with what real runs on Bolt Brawl's mechs showed: `chop` came back the same motion as `slash`; `punch`, `kick`, `defeat_01` and `strafe` aren't presets (box, front_kick, defeat_02 and nothing: `walk` is forward only, so strafe, back-steps, heavy landings and lunges are done in code on the rig); and the mocap is long and mostly filler (`idle` 15.4 s, `hurt` 13.9 s, `shoot` 9.1 s, `slash` 6.6 s with the wind-up at 0.6–1.8 s, the strike at ≈ 2.0 s and the settle by 3.4 s). Keep the part you use with `--clip`: `--clip Idle=0..4,Slash=0.6..3.4`, or split one into beats, `--clip Windup=Slash@0.6..1.8,Strike=Slash@1.8..3.4`. Trimming happens when mb processes the download, so it's free and `--reprocess` can change it.

Why Tripo first: it is the only API among those checked (Meshy, Tripo, Hyper3D Rodin, fal.ai's Hunyuan3D and Trellis) that rigs and animates non-humanoids, so a fish can swim and a dog can walk, not only people; its P1 model is made for low-poly game assets with a face limit; it's pay as you go at $0.01 a credit with no subscription, and paid credits' outputs are yours. Meshy's humanoid pipeline is strong (walk and run come with the rig, a large clip library), but its API needs a $20/month plan and can't rig animals. Rodin has no rigging and needs a $120/month plan for API access.

Licenses: Tripo gives paid users all rights to their outputs; what free credits make stays Tripo's, so don't ship those. Meshy's paid plans own their assets; the free plan's are CC BY 4.0. The sidecar notes which.

**Setting up Tripo:** sign up at [platform.tripo3d.ai](https://platform.tripo3d.ai), create a key under API Keys, buy credits (Billing; $10 is 1000 credits, about a dozen animated models), then `mb keys set tripo` or `export TRIPO_API_KEY=tsk_…`. **Meshy:** a Pro plan or above at [meshy.ai](https://www.meshy.ai/pricing), then a key at [meshy.ai/developers/keys](https://www.meshy.ai/developers/keys) and `mb keys set meshy` (or `MESHY_API_KEY`).

#### 3D models on Maimbrain's key

If the Maimbrain team has also given your account 3D access (`mb models` marks the Tripo models `M`), `mb model` runs on Maimbrain's Tripo key when you have no Tripo key of your own (or with `--provider maimbrain`), within the same monthly budget as art and sound. Everything works as on your own key: rigging, clips, `--rig` on a model made earlier, `--variants`. mb's Tripo client sends each step to the Maimbrain server (`{server}/api/v1/generate/tripo/…`, signed in with your `mb login` token), which starts the task on Tripo with its key; mb polls it and downloads the result from Tripo as usual. Meshy isn't covered.

- **Cost:** each task is charged at its list price when it starts (model $0.40 on P1, $0.20 on H3.1, +$0.10 from an image; rig $0.25; clip $0.10; the rig check is free), then at what Tripo reports when it finishes; a task Tripo couldn't make costs nothing. mb checks its estimate for the whole run against what's left before it starts, instead of the account balance.
- **How many at once:** two running tasks of each kind (P-series, H-series, rigging and clips) per person, and Tripo's own limits for everyone together. A run over either waits and tries again, as it does when a Tripo account is busy (`tripo: 2 of your p-series tasks are already running on Maimbrain's Tripo key; trying again in 5 s`), so parallel `mb model` runs are fine; they just queue.
- **Tasks belong to an account.** A model made on your own key was made on your Tripo account, and Maimbrain's can't rig it from its task, nor add clips to its rig (and the other way round). The sidecar records `"via": "maimbrain"` on each task made on Maimbrain's key; `--rig` on a model from the other account uploads its raw `.glb` instead, and new clips for a rig from the other account rig it again.
- The server runs the originality check on the prompt; reference images and `.glb` files up to 20 MB are uploaded through it.

**How many at once:** a Tripo account runs at most **5 P-series** (P1) and **10 H-series** generation tasks at the same time (each category has its own pool; rigging and clips share an animation pool of 10), whichever of its keys starts them; one more is refused (HTTP 429, code 2000), and many requests in a few seconds are refused with code 1007. `mb model` queues for you: before it creates a generation task it takes one of the pool's slots, a locked file in mb's config directory (`~/.config/maimbrain/tripo-slots/`; the OS frees it if `mb` dies), so a seventh `mb model … &` on one machine waits (`tripo: 5 p-series tasks are already running from mb on this machine …; waiting for one to finish`) and starts when one finishes. A creation the server still refuses (tasks started from another machine or the web console) is retried after 5, 10, 20, 40, then every 60 s, up to `MB_MODEL_TIMEOUT` (900 s). So running six parts in parallel is fine: five run, the sixth starts when the first is done. **The balance:** before generating on your own key, `mb model` asks Tripo for the account's spendable credits (`GET /user/balance`; credits held by running tasks don't count) and refuses a run it can't cover, saying how much is missing (`tripo: the account has $0.40 (40 credits) and this needs about $0.80 (80 credits): $0.40 (40 credits) short. Top up at https://developers.tripo3d.ai …`); a balance it can't read is a note, and the run goes ahead. Parallel runs each check, and a running task's credits are already held, so a batch that would run out stops at the first run that can't be paid for instead of failing halfway through a model.

How a run goes: each step is an asynchronous task on the provider (Tripo: generate, check whether it can be rigged, rig, one retarget per clip; Meshy: a shape preview, the texture refine, the rig, one animation per clip). mb polls each every 3 s and prints its progress; a model takes 1–5 minutes, longer with clips. `MB_MODEL_TIMEOUT` (seconds, default 900) is how long to wait for one task. Download links last minutes (Tripo) to days (Meshy), so mb downloads each result as soon as it's ready. If the model is made but rigging or a clip fails, you keep the model, the run says why, and the sidecar's `result.notes` records it; the task ids are in `result.tasks` for a support request.

**Prompts:** describe what you want, positively. Generators read "NO arms", "without a gun" as a request for arms and a gun (the words are there); "a smooth armless torso, a rounded capsule body" works better, and `--negative "arms, weapons"` (Tripo's negative prompt) is the place for what to leave out. A part generated alone ("a mech arm") can come back as a whole mech: name the part's shape and say it's a single part ("a single robotic forearm, cylindrical, ending in a three-fingered claw, nothing attached").

**Parts and `--orient`.** Generated parts come back in whatever pose the generator liked (Bolt Brawl's beam saber lay along a 3D diagonal, an armor plate leaned back 45°), so each had to be worked out from its mesh (its `socket_fit.py`). mb does that now. It reads the rest pose's triangles and finds their area-weighted principal axes; the **tip** is the long axis's slimmer end (the cross-sections of the last fifth at each end), or, when the ends are alike, the end farther from the widest slice (a blade's guard); for a blocky part (a gun with a big body) it uses the model's own longer horizontal axis, since guns come back level. The **face** is the thin axis's outer side: the side its glow is on, else the convex side (where the middle stands proud of the rim). `--orient tip=+y` turns the tip to +y and the flat side to +z; `--orient tip=+z up=+y` turns it to +z keeping the model's own up as near +y as it goes; `--orient face=+z` puts the face on +z, top up. A close call (ends or sides alike) is a warning: look at the preview and flip the axis's sign (`tip=-y`). The sidecar's `result.orient` has what was found and the turn. A `--kind part` prompt also asks for a plain rest pose that suits `--orient` ("shown standing straight upright, its point straight up and its flat side toward the viewer, not tilted"); a real run of a beam saber came back upright and needed only a quarter turn about +y for its flat side.

**`mb model info <glb>`** prints that analysis for any `.glb` (one mb wrote, a raw provider output in `art/raw/`, another tool's): size and bounds, the three principal axes with their extents, the shape (long, flat or a block), the tip and how it was told, the widest slice, the face and how it was told, the glow spot (the middle and radius of the glowing triangles: a weak spot to aim at), the anchor (the middle, which `--origin center` puts on the origin) and the offset to give the model under a socket's node, and which `--orient` suits it with the quaternion it would apply. A part is centered on the origin by default, so its socket offset is zero.

**Facing:** mb turns a model to face −z as games expect. For a long creature it checks that the thicker end (the head) is in front and turns it round if not; a close call, and any squat or round shape (a mech, a crate, a lamp) where there's no long axis to judge by, is reported `facing uncertain` (and `facing_check.verdict: "uncertain"` in the sidecar): check the preview's arrow and `--turn 90`, `180` or `-90`.

### What mb does to the model

`crates/mb-cli/src/generate/model.rs`, with `glb.rs` (reading and writing), `shape.rs` (the processing) and `raster.rs` (the preview):

1. **Read** the provider's `.glb` and every clip's `.glb`: triangle meshes (also strips and fans, any attribute encoding, sparse accessors), PBR materials, PNG and JPEG textures, nodes, skins, morph targets and animations. What mb3d doesn't read is dropped and listed: other extensions (`KHR_materials_specular`…), occlusion maps, second UV sets, cameras, lights. A file that needs a decoder (Draco, meshopt, KTX2, WebP) is refused with a message.
2. **Clips**: each clip's channels are copied onto the rigged model by bone name and the clip is named for the action (`swim` → `Swim`).
3. **Triangles**: vertices that are identical are welded, then meshoptimizer's simplifier (`meshopt` crate) brings each mesh to its share of `--polys`, steered by normals and UVs so shading and texture seams hold; skin weights and morph targets ride along. Vertex caches are optimized. Morph targets past mb3d's 4 are dropped; missing normals are computed.
4. **Glow** (`--glow`): an emissive map from the base color's bright texels, with `KHR_materials_emissive_strength`; with `--glow-color`, from the texels near those colors instead (the map replaces any emissive map the provider sent). Pick the color from the texture itself (`art/raw/<name>-<seed>.glb`'s base color), not from the prompt: "a lime gem" came back `#5bca2b` on Bolt Brawl's plate, and `--glow-color '#5bca2b'` lit 3.0 % of it, the gem alone, where `--glow` lit the pearl-white plate all over.
5. **Rig safety**: a skin over mb3d's 64 joints loses leaf joints, merged into their parents (a vertex weighted to a leaf follows its parent), instead of drawing unskinned; a rig whose joints on one side have no mirror on the other (one wing, three stubs) is called out with the `--spine` fix. Joints named for a side (`L_Thigh`/`R_Thigh` as Tripo names them, `LeftUpLeg` as Mixamo does, `thigh.L`, `thigh_l`, `wing_l_0`) are paired by name, wherever the bones point; a rig whose names don't say is mirrored by position across x. The sidecar's `result.rig` has the joint count, what was merged, `symmetric` and the joint tree with the vertices each joint carries. Tripo's avian rig has no clips: use `--spine` and `Flight`.
6. **Place**: every root goes under one new node named after the asset that turns the model from the provider's front (Tripo and Meshy both deliver glTF's +z) to `--facing`, scales its rest pose's longest side to `--size` meters and moves it onto the ground or the origin. Bones and clips are untouched, so `node_find` and sockets work; the nodes that draw are renamed `<asset>/body`, `<asset>/body2`… (providers name them `tripo_node_<uuid>`, new every take), so `model.find("heron/body")?.material()` reaches the mesh in every take. **Facing check** for long creatures (length ≥ 1.8× width and height): the head is taken to be the thicker end (cross-section over the last 15 %); clearly backwards (1.3× or more) and no `--turn` given, it is turned 180° and the run says so; a close call warns "facing uncertain". The verdict is in `result.facing_check`. **A rigged biped faces where its rig says**, whatever its shape: its toes point forward from its ankles, and its `L_`/`R_` joints put its right side to its right (forward = up × right, up from the hips to the head). When the two agree that settles it (`facing_check.verdict` `rig`, with `rig_forward`, the axis it came back facing): Tripo's rigged mechs come back facing +x, not +z, so without a `--turn` mb turns them by the rig and says so (`--turn 360` keeps the old way, for a game that already turns them in code); with a `--turn`, it says where the rig ends up facing if that isn't `--facing`. Squat mechs were "facing uncertain: not a long shape" before. `mb model info` prints the rig's forward too.
7. **Spine** (`--spine`): the placed mesh is baked into the model's space (the root is the identity), a chain of joints is laid along the body's long axis in the side plane (along z for a fish or a serpent, near vertical for a bird sculpted standing with its wings spread), each at its slice's centroid so it follows a raised neck or a curled tail, head at the end that reaches furthest forward; each vertex is weighted over the nearest joints with a Gaussian (σ ≈ 0.6 joint) so it bends smoothly. With wings (`auto` adds them for an avian rig type or a bird word in the prompt, when the model is about as wide as it is long), a symmetric shoulder and elbow on each side from the two sides' averaged wing bands, and vertices past the shoulder blend into them by distance from the body. The sidecar's `result.spine` lists the bones and where they are.
8. **Textures**: `--metal` and `--min-roughness` first (the metalness factor scaled; the roughness floor written into the metal-rough map's green channel, since roughness is that map times the factor and the factor is already 1: in game code `d.metallic *= 0.15` works on a loaded material, but `d.roughness = d.roughness.max(0.6)` changes nothing, which is why the floor belongs here). The sidecar's `result.metal_rough` and `metal_rough_from_provider` have the averages. Then resized: the base color and emissive maps to `--texture-size`, the normal and metal-rough maps to `--maps-size` (Lanczos), JPEG at quality 85 (92 for normal and metal-rough maps) or PNG where alpha is used; if the file would pass 8 MB they're halved until it fits. Bolt Brawl's hauler with 1024² color and 512² maps: 931 → 595 KB.
9. **Compress** (unless `--no-compress`): the geometry is written quantized (`KHR_mesh_quantization`, which mb3d reads in the web runtime and on iOS alike: both load glTF through mb-host's `mb3d/gltf.rs`): positions as normalized 16-bit integers in each mesh's bounding cube, normals and tangents as 8-bit, UVs as 16-bit when they lie in 0–1, vertex colors as 8-bit, skin weights as 16-bit and joints as 8-bit, rotation keys as 16-bit. The offset and scale that put the positions back go on the node that draws the mesh (or, for a skinned mesh, into its skin's inverse bind matrices); a mesh with morph targets keeps float positions. The geometry takes about half the bytes with no visible change (16 bits over a 5 m mech is a 0.15 mm step), which matters because a bundle's deflate barely shrinks float geometry: on Bolt Brawl's hauler mech (5.7 k triangles, three 1024² maps) 391 → 213 KB of geometry, the file 1105 → 931 KB (deflated 916 → 859 KB); its shoulder gun 67 → 39 KB, its chest plate 60 → 34 KB. The run prints both sizes; the sidecar has `bytes` and `bytes_uncompressed`. One thing to know: the node that draws a compressed mesh carries that offset and scale, so move or turn a part by a node above it (the spawn node, a socket), not with `find("<asset>/body").set_transform(…)`, which would replace them (or pass `--no-compress`).
10. **Check**: the validator's `.glb` structure check (`mb_format::bundle::check_glb`), a read-back, textures ≤ 2048², warnings for more than 64 joints in a skin (mb3d would draw it unskinned), more than 30 k triangles, or a file over 3 MB.
11. **Write** `assets/<name>.glb`, its sidecar (the prompt, provider, model, seed, cost, triangles before and after, textures, joints, clips with lengths, size in meters, what was dropped, notes, task ids, and the raw files) and the raw outputs in `art/raw/` (`<name>-<seed>.glb`, `<name>-<seed>.<action>.glb`), so `mb regen assets/<name>.glb --reprocess` redoes steps 1–9 offline after you change `--polys`, `--size` or the texture options in the recipe.

**Reprocessing keeps everything.** `mb model <game> <name> --reprocess [options]` and `mb regen <asset> --reprocess` start from the recorded recipe (kind, glow colors and tolerance, compression, texture and map sizes, `--metal`/`--min-roughness`, `--orient`, placement), change only the options given, and write the result back where the model is: `--out` names the model to reprocess (default `assets/<name>.glb`), and a model moved since it was made (made with `--out art/pending/…`, then moved into `assets/` with its sidecar) is written where it is now, not where it was made, and its recipe's `out` is updated. So nothing has to be redone by hand after a reprocess.
12. **Preview**: `art/preview/<name>.png`, a contact sheet from a small software renderer: the rest pose from the front, front-right, right, back, left and above, with a grid under it and an orange arrow on the ground pointing the way it should face, then a row of six frames per clip. If the arrow and the head disagree, regenerate with `--turn 90` (or `-90`, `180`) or `--reprocess` after editing the sidecar's `turn`. Then look at it in the game with `mb shot`.

In the game: `Model::load(asset)` once the asset is `Ready`, `model.spawn(parent)`, and play clips with `gfx3d::Animator` (`model.clip("Swim")`), as in the skill's 3D section. Clips are made in place (no root motion): move the model's root node yourself.

**Bones for a model that came back static or with a bad rig.** `mb model games/koigate dragon --import assets/dragon.glb --spine` (or `--reprocess --spine` with the raw output) gives it a spine; then `Skeleton::spine(node)` right after `spawn` adopts the bones, and `Swim` (a fish), `Swim` with `wave.axis = Vec3::X` (a dragon undulating in flight) or `Flight` (a bird) move it like a skeleton built in code. `games/mb3d-demo` (`--param scene=creatures`) flies a generated heron and dragon and swims a koi this way.


Sections: `[sprite]`, `[frames]`, `[portrait]`, `[background]`, `[layer]`, `[tile]`, `[ui]`, `[icon]`, `[sky]`, `[music]`, `[sfx]`, `[3d]`, `[3d_object]`. Every prompt is built the same way: what the asset is (per kind), the kind's words, `style`, `keywords`, the pixel-art grid, the palette, how to use the reference images, the background rule for transparent assets, what to avoid, and "an original design". `--dry-run` shows it. Each sidecar records the style file's hash, and `mb art check` flags assets made with an older version of it (`mb regen` refreshes them).

Once one asset looks right (usually the hero), add it to `references`: everything after it matches it.

## Post-processing

Images (`crates/mb-cli/src/generate/art.rs`, `img.rs`):

1. **Request**: the nearest supported aspect ratio to the target (`1:1`, `2:3`, `3:2`, `3:4`, `4:3`, `4:5`, `5:4`, `9:16`, `16:9`, `21:9`; frames are asked for in a near-square grid), at `1K` (backgrounds and layers `2K`), with the style references first and `--ref` photos last.
2. **Transparency** (sprites, frames, UI, near layers): the prompt asks for one flat `key` color (by default whichever of magenta, green, cyan or blue is farthest from the palette). mb estimates the real background from the image border, clears pixels close to it, computes each edge pixel's alpha by projecting its color between the background and the nearest solid subject color (which also removes the background's tint, "despill"), and drops specks. A provider's real alpha (OpenAI) is kept as is. Warnings say when the background wasn't flat or the subject touches the edges.
3. **Fit**: sprites and UI are trimmed and scaled into the exact size with a margin, centered or bottom-anchored; backgrounds and icons are cropped to the aspect ratio and resized (Lanczos, in premultiplied alpha).
4. **Frames**: the sheet is split into its connected shapes (small bits join the nearest frame) in reading order, falling back to an even grid (with a warning only when the grid's column lines cut through the drawings in the top half of the rows, where the heads are: busts whose shoulders meet are how portrait sheets come back, and split cleanly); every frame gets the same scale and is anchored the same way in equal cells. Talking portraits are aligned to their first cell ([below](#talking-portraits-mb-art-portrait)).
5. **Pixel art** (`--grid` or `pixel_art`): the content is fitted at 8 texels per art pixel, each cell becomes opaque when at least half covered and takes the median color of its middle (so a model's blended "pixels" don't muddy it), then it's scaled up by whole texels.
6. **Seamless** (tiles both ways, layers horizontally): the image is made a band wider, and the band is joined onto the opposite edge at the column (row) where the two match best, with a short blend.
6b. **Skies** (`mb art sky`, `sky.rs`): see [Skies](#skies-mb-art-sky) below.
7. **Color**: `palette_lock` snaps to the palette (Oklab distance), `colors`/`--colors` quantize (median cut, alpha included).
8. **PNG**: the smallest of indexed (at 1, 2, 4 or 8 bits, with tRNS), RGB and RGBA encodings with several filters at maximum compression. With `--max-kb`, it quantizes to 256, 128, … colors until the file fits; otherwise it warns about big files.

Audio (`sound.rs`, `audio.rs`):

1. Decode (WAV, MP3, Ogg or raw PCM), mix to mono, resample (windowed sinc) to `--rate`.
2. **Loops**: detect the tempo from the onset envelope's autocorrelation (or use `--bpm`); fit the largest whole number of 4/4 bars up to `--bars` after skipping the intro and the last second; search start points where what follows the loop's end best matches what follows its start (loudness contour over ~1 s, then waveform at sample level within ±0.4 % of the length); bake the bar that followed the loop's end into its start (an equal-power crossfade, `--loop-xfade` bars, default 1, shorter if the take ends sooner), so the file repeats seamlessly at its exact length. The log and sidecar give the loop's length, bars, the BPM implied by the final length (use it for a beat grid), where it starts in the source and the seam check. Without a steady beat, `--seconds` cuts a free-length loop the same way.
3. **Key**: the music's key, measured as `mix_check.py` does (power per pitch class from 55 Hz to 2 kHz; the key whose scale holds the most of it, ties between relative keys broken by the Krumhansl profiles), in the log and the sidecar (`key`, `key_fit`). Lyria ignores the key it's asked for.
4. **Layers** (`--layers`): a Linkwitz-Riley low-pass at `--split-hz`, run over the loop twice so its seam is seamless, gives `_base`; `_hi` is the rest, so base + high = the full mix. Both share one gain. With `tension`, the [tension kit](#tension-layers) too.
5. **Effects**: trim leading and trailing silence (3 ms pre-roll, 8 ms fade), or cross-fade the end into the start for `--loop`. With `--brighten`, below.
6. **Levels**: the same rules as `sfx.py`: RMS of the loud two thirds to −20 dBFS (music) or −14 dBFS (effects), a look-ahead limiter holding peaks under −3 dBFS (for loops, limited as if on repeat, so the gain agrees across the seam).
7. **Encode** to Ogg Vorbis at `--quality` (libvorbis with the aoTuV patches, built into mb, so nothing else is installed; `mb ogg` exposes the same encoder), decode the result, and re-encode quieter if Vorbis pushed a peak past −1 dBFS; report length, peak, loudness, the loop seam (as `check_audio.py` does) and how much a phone speaker loses of it (below). If encoding fails, the processed WAV is kept in `art/raw/`.

### Heard on a phone

A phone speaker barely plays below ~300 Hz, and ElevenLabs happily returns bass-heavy takes ("a deep plunge", "a soft deep gong") that it loses 10–30 dB of. That used to surface only when `mix_check.py` measured a bot round, long after the sound was made. Now each file's loss is measured as it's written, with mix_check's phone model (K-weighting, then a 4th-order roll-off at 300 Hz): over an effect's loudest 50 ms (what mix_check measures of each play) or a loop's whole length. It's in the log (`phone -12.0 dB`) and the sidecar (`phone_loss_db`; `mix_phone_loss_db` for a whole track), and `check_audio.py` prints the same number. Past −10 dB, `mb sfx` warns and suggests a brighter prompt or `--brighten`; `mb music` suggests brighter instruments and a lighter low end.

`--brighten` ("virtual bass"): the part of the effect under ~250 Hz is bent through a soft clipper and a rectifier, which makes its harmonics (2×, 3×, 4× … its frequency); those a phone can play (280 Hz–4 kHz) are added back, the least that brings the loss within 6 dB (from 5 % of the bass's level up), and rumble under 35 Hz is cut. The ear hears a missing fundamental from its harmonics, so a deep thud still reads as deep on a phone and sounds as before on headphones. It's in the recipe (`brighten`), its before and after in the sidecar (`result.brighten`); `mb regen <asset> --reprocess --brighten` applies it to a take you have, for free, `--no-brighten` takes it off, and the raw take stays in `art/raw/`. A sound that is already bright enough is left alone.

### Tension layers

`--layers base,hi,tension` adds four files for music that builds anxiety as a threat closes in (the recipe that uses them: the skill's SKILL.md "Music that follows the game"):

| File | What | Level |
|---|---|---|
| `<name>_tension.ogg` | loop: a drone on the root and fifth, breathing over two bars; an eighth-note pulse on the root, accents on the beat; a heartbeat (lub-dub) on each bar's 1; string tremolo in sixteenths on two in-key notes a semitone apart (the fifth and minor sixth in a minor key, the seventh and octave in major), swelling over each 4-bar phrase; a riser (swept noise and a rising glide) through the last bar of each phrase | loud RMS −23 dBFS, 3 dB under the full mix |
| `<name>_tick.ogg` | loop: a clock, tick on even eighths, tock on odd, ghost sixteenths, the bar's 1 accented | 10 dB under the tension layer |
| `<name>_heartbeat.ogg` | one-shot: a lub-dub, with harmonics a phone can play | effects' level (−14 dBFS) |
| `<name>_sting.ogg` | one-shot, 1.6 s: a low boom, a string stab on the root and the rubbing pair whose filter closes as it dies, a burst of noise | effects' level |

They're **synthesized by mb to fit the loop**, not generated. Lyria returns one finished stereo mix per request: no stems, no audio to follow, and a second request with a "tense" prompt comes back in another key and at another tempo (its seed doesn't hold either across prompts). Lining that up with the loop would mean time-stretching and pitch-shifting it, which smears it, and its groove still wouldn't sit with the first. What the loop does give exactly is its length (a whole number of bars), its beats and its key, and dread is mostly rhythm and register, which synthesize well:

- **Length and tempo**: both loops are exactly as many samples as `_base`, and every oscillator runs a whole number of cycles per loop (frequencies move by under a cent), every envelope and filter is periodic in the loop, and the limiter runs as if on repeat: they loop with no seam and stay sample-aligned with the base when started with one `at`.
- **Phase**: the beats are found in the loop (the onset envelope folded on the beat, weighting the lows, where kicks are; the downbeat is the beat of the bar with the strongest low-end onsets). The log and sidecar give `downbeat_seconds` and `beat_confidence`; under 0.15 it warns. A loop without a steady beat (`--seconds`) gets its own pulse of about two beats a second, with a warning.
- **Key**: the measured key (step 3), or `--key`. The drone, pulse and heartbeat sit on the root; the rubbing pair is in the scale, so the layer clashes with itself a semitone apart, not with the track (mix_check's key check passes), and both its notes are also in the relative major or minor, which a key estimate often confuses.

Their sidecars say `"provider": "mb"`, `"model": "tension-kit"`, cost 0, `result.synthesized`, and carry the generated loop's provenance (prompt, raw, seed); `mb regen` on any of them regenerates the whole set.

How loud to play them is in SKILL.md and the SDK's `music::Director` (below): the tension layer up to vol 1.4 at full danger, level with the track (its file sits 3 dB under it), the clock to 1.0, the heartbeat from 0.15 to 0.3. That lifts the music by 3–5 dB on a phone at full danger over a typical take, which `mix_check.py` now asks for (assets.md §5). A louder, brighter take needs the kit louder (a creator's take at −22 LUFS short-term was lifted only +0.3 dB, and took the layer and the clock at 3.0 and the heartbeat at 0.4–0.8 to reach +3.8), so with a music graph `mb music` measures it: the take's power over 3 s windows as a phone plays it (K-weighted, rolled off under 300 Hz: mix_check's model), its median, and the kit's at full danger (the layer, the clock, and the heartbeat on three beats a bar), give the factor on those levels that lifts the median by +4.5 dB, at least 1 and at most where the layer reaches vol 4. It's the graph's `levels.tension_gain` (with the lift it predicts, `tension_lift_phone_db`), printed in the log, and `Levels::for_graph` applies it. A test plays the Director over a real chase take this way and renders it as mix_check does (`generate/tension_mix.rs`), so the levels can't drift from what mix_check passes. At the levels the skill gave before (1.0, 0.9, 0.1–0.2), a 30 s danger ramp over a Lyria take lifted it by only 2.3–2.6 dB, and a heartbeat at 0.1 failed mix_check's phone check while the danger hovered mid-way (a creator's journal, F4). The sting is brightened like the heartbeat (its boom's harmonics), so a phone plays it.

### Music in sections

One 8-bar loop gets old by the second minute, and a player hears the same bars whatever the game does. `--variations`, `--sections` and `--zones` ask for **one piece** in sections, and write it as a **music graph**: the take itself, and how a game moves through it without a loop anyone can hear (the way shipped games do adaptive music, below):

| Option | Zones | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `--variations 4` | one, named after the music (`v1` … `v4` its sections) | the same groove, a new melody and arrangement each, heard in turn |
| `--sections a,b,a,bridge` | one per distinct section (a repeated name is the same zone, in two places of the take), and a stinger into each, `_into_<section>` | a form to move through |
| `--zones "lantern town: warm lanterns, a slow pulse; bamboo grove: hollow wood, shakuhachi"` | one per zone, and a stinger into each, `_into_<zone>` | a theme per place; with `--variations 2`, two sections per zone (`<zone>_1`, `_2`; the stinger goes into `_1`) |

The files: `<name>_take.ogg` (with `--layers base,hi`, `<name>_take_base.ogg` and `<name>_take_hi.ogg`, which add up to the take), the take as one file from its start to its last section's post-exit; `<name>.music.json`, its graph; the stingers; `<name>_swell`, a one-beat swell that lands on the bar, the cover for a change without a stinger; and with `tension`, one [tension kit](#tension-layers) on the piece's grid. The take's sidecar has the shared facts (`bpm`, `bars`, the piece's `grid`, `key`, `sections`, `mismatches`, `graph`); a stinger's or the swell's has `land_seconds`, where its hit is. The SDK's `maimbrain::music::Director::from_graph` plays them (SKILL.md "Music that follows the game"). `--loops` also cuts a loop of each section that repeats on its own, the way before the graph ([below](#loops---loops)), for `Director::new`; the take is 2.3 MB as two layers for a 3-minute piece (a third more than 16 loops of 16 s), and 66 MB decoded on the device (the loops: 49 MB).

#### The music graph

A generative model writes music that develops rather than loops: cutting a loop out of a take joins weakly (half the loops of the demo take below joined under 0.85 even with a bar's crossfade baked in and the best window of each section), and writing a 4-bar phrase twice to make one halves the music there is. Games solved this long ago; the graph does what they do, on the take as it is (graph.rs):

1. **Segments with a pre-entry and a post-exit** (Wwise and FMOD practice). Each section of the form is a segment of the take: its body from its entry cue (a bar line) to its exit cue, with the take's own audio around it, a beat before the entry as its pre-entry (the pickup into it) and a bar after the exit as its post-exit (its tail). A section's entry is where the request put it, moved up to two bars onto the bar line where the music changes most (the two bars before against the two after). On a change, the new segment starts so that its entry lands on the old one's exit cue: its pre-entry fades in under the last beat (a quarter sine) and the old one's post-exit dies away over it (60 dB over the bar, like a released note: about −15 dB a beat in). Nothing is baked into the take.
2. **Changes only at exit cues** (horizontal re-sequencing). Exit cues end each 4-bar phrase, counted from the section's entry, and its exit; every bar line is an intermediate cue for an urgent change. The graph's `transitions` table says, for each pair of zones, whether the take itself runs from one into the other, at how many of the first zone's cues a seamless link (score ≥ 0.8) goes into the second, the best one, and what covers a change without one (the new zone's stinger, else the swell).
3. **Jump links** (Paul Lamere's Infinite Jukebox: split a track into beats, fingerprint each, and jump between near-identical ones to play forever with no loop point). The whole take is analysed beat by beat on its one grid: pitch-class profiles (harmony), levels in eight bands per half beat (timbre and loudness), and the onset envelope (rhythm). Two bar lines are linked when the bar before each and the bar after each match on all three (`score`, with `harmony`, `level_db` and `rhythm`: the same measure as `loop_match` below). Jumps are only bar to bar, so the beat of the bar is kept. Each zone's links start at 0.85, and the threshold comes down by 0.025 to 0.7 at the least until the zone has 6 that aren't short repeats (a jump back of fewer than 8 bars); links between zones are kept from 0.65, the best three per bar line and zone, as ways into another zone (or into the two bars the take leads into one of its sections with).
4. **The way back** (`returns`): where a zone's stretch of the take ends, the music jumps back into the zone by a link in its last phrase, or else the zone starts again from its entry under the swell.

The Director plays the take straight through and takes a link now and then (seeded, so a replay hears the same music), and when the game asks for another zone it gets there at the next good point: through a seamless link if one comes first, the take's own transition if it runs there, or at the next exit cue under the stinger, at most a bar after the first bar line it could have used. The base and high layers jump together, and the tension kit follows the take's phrase.

`<name>.music.json` (version 1): `files` (the take or its layers, the swell, the kit, paths from the game's root), `grid` (`bpm`, `bar_seconds`, `first_bar_seconds`: bar k starts at `first_bar_seconds + k × bar_seconds` in the take; `phrase_bars`, the usable `first_bar` and `end_bar`), `splice` (how links are spliced: a quarter beat of pre-entry, a beat of post-exit), `zones` (sections, `link_threshold`, `stinger`), `sections` (`entry_bar`, `exit_bar`, `exit_cues`, `segment`: `pre_entry_seconds`, `entry_seconds`, `exit_seconds`, `post_exit_seconds`), `transitions`, `returns`, `links` (`from`, `to`, `score` and its parts, `into_zone` for a link into another zone), `risks`, and with the tension kit `levels` (`tension_gain`, how much louder than the Director's defaults the kit plays over this take, [above](#tension-layers); optional, 1 when it's missing). The log prints, per section, its links and the threshold, each pair of zones and each zone's way back.

On the demo take (a chase in four zones × 2 variations, 172 s, 84 bars at 120 BPM in F minor): 7 of 8 entries moved a bar onto the change. Of 1538 pairs of bar lines within a zone, a typical one scores 0.2–0.5 (Lyria keeps developing: harmony 0.2–0.4); 26 links passed the zones' thresholds (lantern town 0.85: 10; bamboo grove 0.70: 2; lotus marsh 0.82: 10; maple gorge 0.70: 4), and 67 go between zones. The town returns by a link (0.94); the other three zones have no link back better than 0.50–0.53 and restart under the swell. The town runs into the grove in the take, and links into it from 4 of its 6 cues (two at 1.00: bars of the grove's second section repeat the town's); the grove links back to the town from 2 of its cues and the gorge into the grove from 1 of 5; every other change at a cue goes under a stinger.

**How it stays in one key and tempo.** Separate requests don't: Lyria ignores the key it's asked for, lands a few BPM off the tempo asked for, and its seed holds neither across prompts. So the sections are asked for in **one** request: one piece at one tempo and key, with a timestamped line per section (`[0:00 - 0:22] Lantern Town: warm lanterns…`), which Lyria 3.5 follows for a couple of minutes (gemini switches to `lyria-3.5` for this; $0.08 a take). Each section is given its loop plus three bars (at least 18 s); that length is recorded (`section_seconds`) so `--reprocess` finds the sections again. Then mb measures (the graph plays the take as it is: corrections apply to `--loops`):

1. **Tempo**: the beat period within ±12 % of the asked tempo (a general tempo detector took a 125 BPM taiko take for 166, 4/3 of it), refined over two bars and then over the whole loop length (to a sample or so), all from how the onsets repeat.
2. **Each section's tempo**: its own two-bar period, in the log. With `--loops`, a section off by more than 0.3 % is time-stretched to the piece's tempo (WSOLA, pitch kept), by up to 5 %; further off is a mismatch.
3. **Key**: each section's key is measured as `mix_check.py` does; the piece's key is the one most sections fit (one section that wandered doesn't pull it), or `--key`. With `--loops`, a section that clashes the way mix_check flags a layer (under 70 % in key, or 8 points worse than a shift) is pitch-shifted when one semitone fixes it; further off is a mismatch.
4. **One grid**: a take is one performance at one tempo, so it has one bar grid, found once over the whole take and recorded in every sidecar (`grid`: `downbeat_in_take_seconds`, `bar_seconds`, how many sections agree on it). The beats come from the onsets of the whole take. Which beat is the 1 is the hard part, since taiko and plucked strings don't put a kick on every 1: chords and melodies change on the 1, so the harmonic change across each beat votes (pitch-class profiles two beats and a bar either side), with the low onsets as a lighter third. The graph's bars are this grid's, and every loop starts on one of its bar lines, 10 ms before it (`downbeat_seconds`; the first hit's attack stays whole), never on a section's own guess. So every jump and every switch on a bar line keeps the bar: even if the grid's 1 were a beat off, all would be off alike. If the 1 is audibly on another beat ([listen](#listening-to-the-jumps-and-changes)), `mb music <game> <name> --downbeat 1` (or `-1`, `2`) moves the piece's grid that many beats and does it all again from the take you have; the recipe keeps it (`downbeat_shift`).
5. **Mismatches**: a section that's missing, or too far off in tempo or key. mb asks again (`--retries`, default 1, at the next seed) and keeps the take with the fewest; any left are in the log, in every sidecar's `mismatches` and in that section's `mismatch`, and the files are still written. Regenerate (`mb regen … --new-seed`) or drop the section.

#### Loops (`--loops`)

`--loops` also writes, per section, a loop that repeats on its own (`<name>_<section>.ogg`, or `_base` + `_hi`), all exactly the same length with a bar's 1 at `downbeat_seconds`, for `Director::new` (each sidecar adds `loop_samples`, `loop_match`, `phrase_bars`, `loop_start_seconds`, `loop_bar`). **Loops that join**: within each section, every window of whole bars on the grid is a candidate, and the one whose end runs into its start most like the music did is cut. A loop of P bars plays bar P−1 into bar 0 where the music played it into bar P, so the join can't be heard when bar P sounds like bar 0: they're compared beat by beat in harmony (pitch-class profiles), level and timbre (eight bands per half beat) and rhythm (the onset envelope). That's `loop_match` (0–1; a groove on the same chord a few bars on scores 0.9–1, the same groove on another chord about 0.5), with its parts in `loop_match_parts`. When no loop of the asked length joins at 0.85 or better and a whole phrase of half of it (4 bars of 8) joins clearly better, the phrase is cut and written twice to fill the length (`phrase_bars`). Then the bar that followed the loop is cross-faded into its start (equal power, corrected for how alike the two are: `--loop-xfade` bars, default 1), so the file repeats seamlessly at its exact length, and the layers are split after that, so `_base` + `_hi` is still the loop and both share its join. The click check runs at the file's ends, where a game wraps it; a step under −40 dBFS (what Vorbis leaves at a smooth layer's ends) isn't called a click.

**A loop of its own for a section that won't join** (`--fix-loops`, which implies `--loops`; off unless given: it costs money). Lyria 3.5 keeps developing a section through its 11 bars, so its best 8-bar loop often joins badly. `--reprocess --fix-loops` (or `--fix-loops` on a new take) asks for one Lyria 3 Clip (30 s, ~$0.04) for each section whose loop joins under 0.85 (`--fix-loops 0.9` for another bar, `1` for every section), prompted with the piece's subject and that section at the piece's tempo and key, made to loop. Each clip is measured and corrected like a section (up to 5 % of tempo, one semitone of key), cut on its own grid (it's another performance), and used only if it joins better than the section's own loop (`source: "clip"`, `clip_raw`, `section_loop_match` in its sidecar). The clips stay in `art/raw/<name>_<section>-<seed>.*`, the recipe lists the ones used (`section_clips`), and `--reprocess` reuses them for free; a clip that didn't fit isn't kept, and asking again tries a new seed. Lyria ignores the key it's asked for, so expect about half the clips to come back too far off in key to use.

In practice (October 2026; a chase in four zones × 2 variations, 120 BPM asked, four takes): one take played at 125 BPM throughout with one section at 120 (stretched by 4 %); one had three sections whose best fit was a key two semitones off (retried); the next fit everywhere, at 120.00 BPM in F minor. Cut freely inside its 22 s sections with a 30 ms crossfade, the loops measured 0.11–0.38 on the join match above (`loop_match` 0.59–0.86 on the measure used then), and a listener heard the joins. On the grid with a bar's crossfade baked in, the same take gives 0.34–0.97, every section as a 4-bar phrase written twice (its best 8-bar loops joined at 0.11–0.84; the 0.34 runs into the take's fade-out). `--fix-loops` asked for six clips ($0.24): three came back 1–4 semitones off in key, one joined worse than its section, two were used (both 1.00, whole 8-bar loops), leaving four of eight under 0.85. On four takes, a section's own vote on the 1 agreed with the whole take's in 28 of 32 sections with harmonic change, 13 of 32 with low onsets alone; the cut before took a section's own guess for one loop (half a beat off the others), and none now does.

#### Listening to the jumps and changes

The numbers can't hear. `mb music <game> <name> --audition` renders, into one file (`art/raw/<name>-audition.ogg`, or a path after the flag), what the Director does with the graph, spliced as it splices (the new voice's pre-entry fading in, the old one's post-exit dying away): every jump link in each zone (two bars of the take up to its bar line, two from where it goes), each zone's restart at its end under the swell, and every change between zones (the best seamless link into the other zone if there is one, else the change at the first zone's first exit cue under the stinger or the swell; and where the take runs from one into the other, that too). A soft click comes before each, and the log prints the timeline with each link's score and parts. The demo take's (26 jumps, 3 restarts, 15 changes) is about six minutes. Music made before the graph (or with the graph file removed) gets the loops' audition instead: each loop's join (its last bar, then its first, as on repeat) and every switch between loops (a bar of the old loop, the new one's stinger or the swell landing on the bar line, two bars of the new loop; a one-bar crossfade between a zone's variations). Listen for the beat, the harmony and the groove carrying on through each one; a jump you can hear is a link to drop (or a reason to try another take), and a 1 on the wrong beat everywhere is `--downbeat`.

**Stingers** (`_into_<zone>`, synthesized like the tension kit, cost 0): a two-beat riser (swept noise, a rising glide, the chord swelling in) landing on a hit, a low boom and a stab on the chord the zone opens with in the take (its entry bar's strongest in-key notes). Play one with `play_at(bar − land_seconds)` and start the zone on that bar; the Director does both. **The swell** (`_swell`): one beat of noise opening upward and the key's root and fifth, landing on a soft hit; no chord, so it covers a change into any zone of the piece, or a zone's restart. The Director covers every change without a seamless link with the new zone's stinger, else the swell, so a listener hears a fill land rather than two pieces of music meeting.

### A different song per zone (`--zone`)

`--zones` makes one piece: every zone in one key and tempo, so the Director can join them seamlessly, and `mb music` warns when a section came back in another key. Sometimes the zones should be different songs on purpose: a boss duel where each boss has their own theme in their own genre, a hub and a dungeon that shouldn't sound related. Then name each zone with its own prompt:

```
mb music games/brawl brawl "instrumental for a cartoon mech-duel boss fight" \
  --zone harbor="bluesy hard rock: overdriven slide guitar, stomping drums, swagger, 128 bpm" \
  --zone neon="flamenco-tango: rasgueado guitar, bandoneon, palmas, dramatic and sly, 112 bpm"
```

Each zone is its own request and its own piece, in its own key and tempo (a "128 bpm" in its prompt is asked for and used for the grid; `--bpm` sets one for the zones that don't say), with everything a piece in sections gets: `brawl_harbor_take.ogg` and its music graph `brawl_harbor.music.json` (16 bars by default, so a fight's worth of music wanders without a loop point), a stinger into it (`brawl_harbor_into_harbor`), the swell and its own tension kit (`_tension`, `_tick`, `_heartbeat`, `_sting`, always), and `--layers`, `--loops`, `--fix-loops`, `--audition` (one file per song) as usual. The positional prompt is optional and added to every zone's. No key check runs between zones (they're meant to differ); each piece is still checked against itself. `brawl.songs.json` lists the songs (zone, graph, the prompt, the tempo asked for and measured, the key), and the log says what each came out as: `2 songs made (harbor: 130 BPM, G minor; neon: 110 BPM, C major), each in its own key and tempo on purpose` (that run: $0.16). One Lyria 3.5 call each ($0.08; up to twice with `--retries 1`).

Zones can be made one run at a time, and a run never loses the others: `brawl.songs.json` is merged, not rewritten, so a zone made again replaces its own entry (in place) and a new one is added at the end (`1 song made (yard: 120 BPM, A minor), listed with the 2 already there (harbor, neon)`). One `--zone` is fine. A zone that fails is reported and the zones after it still generate; the run ends with a summary of the failures, exits non-zero, and gives the command that makes each failed zone again on its own. When a provider's content filter refused the prompt (which happens with harmless genre words: "stomping", "greedy swagger"), it names the words that likely tripped it and the retry command has plainer ones in their place (`heavy`, `brash confident`); ElevenLabs' own rewrite is printed when it sends one. A take no loop could be cut from is a bad take, asked again while `--retries` last; then once more with a longer music model (gemini: Lyria 3.5) or, where there's none, half the bars and a new seed. With a model that always makes 30 s (Lyria 3 Clip through Vertex or Maimbrain's keys, `lyria-002`), 16 bars at a fast tempo don't fit one clip, so the bars drop to what does (12 or 8) unless `--bars` is given.

In the game, `music::Songs` plays them: a `Director` per song (`Setlist::parse` the list, `Director::from_graph` each graph), one at a time. `songs.go(next, Change::Auto, now)` lets the song playing reach its next exit cue (the end of a phrase), lets it die away under the next song's stinger from half a beat before that bar line, and starts the next song from its top on it, so a change of genre lands as a fill and a downbeat rather than a crossfade between two keys. `Change::NextBar` changes on the next bar line instead, `Change::Crossfade(bars)` without the stinger. `songs.update(now, dt, mood)` drives the playing song's layers and tension kit; `near_miss` and `stop` as on a `Director`. `sdk/maimbrain/examples/songs_switch.rs` writes a `mix_check.py` log of a change between two songs to listen to.

### New layers for a take you have

The take a provider returned stays in `art/raw/`, so new layers cost nothing and keep the music you chose. A creator who wanted the tension kit on a track they already had found that generating again with the same seed gave a different take, and edited the sidecar's recipe by hand (F1). Now:

```sh
mb music games/fish chase --reprocess --layers base,hi,tension   # the recorded recipe, the layers given now
mb regen games/fish/assets/chase.ogg --add-layers tension         # the same, from any file it wrote
```

`mb music --reprocess` takes `--layers`, `--key`, `--bars` and `--bpm`, and no prompt. `mb regen --layers` replaces the recorded layers and `--add-layers` adds to them; without `--new-seed`, `--seed`, `--prompt`, `--provider` or `--model` they work from the take you have, and say so. `art/raw/` isn't committed, so on a fresh clone they ask you to generate again.

## Talking portraits (`mb art portrait`)

A character who talks (a rival pilot taunting you, a shopkeeper) needs a face whose mouth moves with the voice line and that blinks now and then. `mb art portrait` makes the whole set as one sheet, so it stays one character:

```sh
mb art portrait games/mechduel rex "a cocky mech pilot: square jaw, gap-toothed grin, dented orange helmet pushed up on his forehead, green flight suit with a lightning-bolt patch" --expressions smug,angry,scared
```

The sheet is **4 columns × (1 + expressions + 1) rows** of equal square cells:

- **Columns** are mouth shapes for lip-sync: 0 closed, 1 slightly open, 2 open, 3 wide open.
- **Rows** are expressions: row 0 neutral, then the ones you name in order (`--expressions smug,angry,scared` gives 1 smug, 2 angry, 3 scared); without `--expressions` it's neutral only.
- **The last row** is the blink: the neutral face with the eyes closed, with the four mouths (so a blink can land mid-word).

The default is 4×2 cells of 128×128 (pixel art: 48×48 art pixels × `pixel_scale`); `--size 96x96` or `--grid 64x64` change the cell, and up to 7 expressions fit. The cell rect for (col, row) is `[col × w, row × h, w, h]`, and the sidecar's `result.portrait` records the layout: `{ "cols": 4, "rows": 5, "cell": [128, 128], "expressions": ["neutral", "smug", "angry", "scared"], "blink_row": 4, "mouths": […] }`. `ui::Portrait` ([UI.md](UI.md)) draws a sheet laid out this way; for pixel art on a grid other than 48 across, tell it with `.art_pixels(n)` (the `rust` line printed says so) so each art pixel lands on whole device pixels.

The prompt asks for the same character in every cell (same head-and-shoulders bust facing the viewer, same position, size, lighting and colors, the shoulders cut off by a straight line, a flat background between cells), with only the mouth, eyes and brows changing; `--dry-run` shows it all. Models still drift a little, so mb aligns the cells to the first one (neutral, mouth closed): each bust is scaled to its width, stood on the cell's bottom edge with its centre of mass over the first's, then nudged a few pixels to where its silhouette (the head counting double) covers the first's best. A cell drawn more than 15% bigger or smaller gets a warning. Pixel art is then reduced to the grid cell by cell and snapped to the palette.

Tips:

- **Pixel art reads best at 48–64 art pixels a cell**: a face needs that many for eyes and four distinct mouths. Below 24 mb warns.
- **Describe the character concretely**: shapes, colors, the one or two things you'd recognize them by ("gap-toothed grin, dented orange helmet"). A named expression works better as a word or two (smug, furious, terrified) than as a sentence.
- **Make a few and pick**: `--variants 3`, look at `art/variants/<name>-sheet.png`, then `mb art pick`. Consistency isn't guaranteed; regenerate when a cell is clearly someone else.
- **Faces that take damage**: `hurt` and `battered` (also `pained`, `wounded`, `beaten`, `bruised`, `damaged`) are spelled out in the prompt (wincing; the same bust bruised, sooty and scratched with a cracked visor), and the prompt lets those rows add their marks on top of the same drawing. `ui::Portrait` uses rows named `hurt` and `battered` for hits and low health (UI.md "Damage and hit reactions").
- **Check the preview** (`art/preview/<name>.png`) column by column: the mouth should open left to right and the head shouldn't move. Use the hero sprite as `--ref` so the portrait and the sprite are the same character.

## Voices (`mb voice`)

Voice acting for characters who talk: barks during play ("Too slow!") and short cutscene lines. A voice is **designed from a description**, never cloned: `mb voice` refuses cloning, impressions and "sounds like <a name>" (a voice is part of a person's likeness), and mb art's names list applies to descriptions and lines. A capitalized word after "like", "voice of" or "voiced by" counts as a name; `--allow-name` lets your own character's name through.

    mb voice design games/duel rocko "a smug rock-star mech pilot in his thirties: raspy, swaggering, a lazy drawl that turns into a showman's yell"
    mb voice pick games/duel rocko 2                       # after listening to art/voices/rocko/preview_*.ogg
    mb voice say games/duel rocko taunt "Is that all you got?" --emotion smug
    mb voice say games/duel rocko ouch "Not the paint job!" --emotion angry --variants 3   # then: mb voice keep games/duel rocko ouch 2
    mb voice script games/duel art/lines.csv --provider mock   # stand-ins with real timings, free, to build the scene
    mb voice script games/duel art/lines.csv                   # the real thing: only lines missing or changed, cost shown first

- **Design** asks ElevenLabs Voice Design (`eleven_ttv_v3`) for three previews of the description, all saying the same sample text (`--sample` for your own, 100–1000 characters; by default the model writes one in character). Previews expire on the provider's side after a day or so: pick soon. Each preview is printed with numbers to pick by, which are also saved with it in `art/voices.toml`: its length (the same text, so a slower voice runs longer), its median pitch in Hz (YIN over the voiced parts: a deep voice ~90–130, a high one 220+; two characters should be far apart), its loudness as delivered (before mb levels it to −16 dBFS) and the phone-speaker loss (below −10 dB is too deep for a phone). `--pick N` keeps one without listening. The cast lives in `art/voices.toml` (description, voice id, seed, which preview; commit it).
- **Lines** use the character's voice with `eleven_v4` (`--model eleven_v3` or `eleven_flash_v2_5` at half the price, flatter, no tags). `--emotion angry|smug|scared|excited|sarcastic|whispering|shouting|laughing…` puts an audio tag in front (`[smug] Is that all you got?`); tags inside the line act too (`"[cackles] Too slow, sweetie!"`), and the subtitle leaves them out. `--delivery creative|natural|robust` (expressive and unsteady → steady; default natural), `--style 0–1`, `--speed 0.7–1.2`, `--variants N`, `--rate`, `--quality`, `--seed`, `--provider mock`. **Speed**: ElevenLabs' `voice_settings.speed` (0.7–1.2) works on Flash, Turbo and Multilingual; Eleven v3 and v4 ignore it (the same seed comes back the same length at any speed), so for them mb asks at normal speed and time-stretches the line after with its pitch kept (WSOLA; clean at these amounts), the subtitle timings and lips with it, and the sidecar's `result.speed` says so. Settings go to the API as two-decimal numbers (an `f32` 0.6 used to go as 0.6000000238418579, which Flash refused).
- **Post-processing** for a phone: mono Ogg, a 90 Hz high-pass (rumble and plosive thumps), +3 dB of presence around 3 kHz (where speech is understood and a phone speaker is loud), trimmed to the words with a breath of room, loudness −16 dBFS (above the music's −20, a little under effects' −14), peaks ≤ −3 dBFS, and the phone-loss check of `mb sfx` (a warning for a voice so deep a phone loses 10 dB of it). Lines over 6 s get a warning.
- **`.lips.txt`** next to each line, for `ui::Portrait` and `ui::Dialogue` ([docs/UI.md](UI.md#talking-portraits-and-dialogue)): the mouth at 30 Hz (`0` closed … `3` wide; from the loudness of the speech band, shut on m/b/p, barely open on f/v and hisses, never wider than `2` on o/u/w) and when each character of the subtitle is spoken, in ms (from the provider's character timings; spread over the voiced part when there are none):

      mblips 1
      hz 30
      dur 1.840
      mouth 0012332100123321000…
      text Is that all you got?
      reveal 0 40 80 120 …

- **Scripts**: a `.csv` (`character,line,text,emotion`, header optional, quotes around commas) or a `.toml` (`[[line]]` with `character`, `id`, `text`, `emotion`). A line is spoken again only when what's sent changes (voice, model, text, emotion, delivery, style, speed, rate, quality: its sidecar's `key`), so editing one line of fifty costs one line. `--force` speaks them all.
- **Sidecars** record the text, what was sent, the voice and model, the timings' source and the cost; `mb regen assets/vo/rocko/taunt.ogg --new-seed` gives another take, `--prompt "…"` changes the line, `--reprocess` redoes the post-processing and the lips from the raw take (free).
- **Prices** (`mb models`): speech $0.08 per 1,000 characters (`eleven_v4`, `eleven_v3`; flash $0.04), so a 40-character bark is about $0.003; a design is about $0.03. ElevenLabs keys have per-feature permissions: the key needs Text to Speech and Voice Generation (Text to Voice) turned on.

- **Running several at once**: an ElevenLabs account runs only so many generation requests at a time (3 on the plan builders hit). Every `mb` run on the machine (`voice`, `sfx`, `music`) queues for those 3 through lock files in mb's config directory (`elevenlabs-slots/`), waiting with a note while all are taken; `MB_ELEVENLABS_CONCURRENCY` sets another plan's limit. A 429 for too many concurrent requests says so. Two runs of the *same* line are refused (`… is being generated by another mb run right now`), and a line's Ogg, `.lips.txt` and sidecar (and its raw take) are written beside it and renamed into place, so a running game or a watcher never reads half a line.

### Voice slots

An ElevenLabs account holds a fixed number of custom voices: 10 on Starter, 30 on Creator, more above. Every voice `mb voice pick` keeps takes one of these slots for as long as it exists, so a builder who designs a cast per game runs out. Premade voices don't take a slot.

    mb voice slots                          # the account, and which games under this repository use which voice
    mb voice slots games/duel games/gallery # look only there

`mb voice slots` reads the account's slot count and its own voices, then finds every `art/voices.toml` under the repository you're in (or the directories given; hidden directories, `target/` and `node_modules/` are skipped) and sorts the voices: **in use** (a character speaks with it, and which), **left behind by an interrupted batch** (temporary voices, named `mb temp …`), and **not used by any game here**. A voice a game here casts that isn't on the account is listed too (another account's key, or deleted). It also shows how many voices were added or edited this month, which ElevenLabs caps separately.

`mb voice design` and `mb voice pick` check first. With no free slot, they stop before anything is spent and say what to do. Previews don't take a slot, so `--ignore-slots` designs anyway and you pick once one is free.

**Releasing a voice.** A game keeps its voice's identity as audio, so the slot can go:

    mb voice release games/duel rocko                 # mark it releasable (it keeps speaking until it's deleted)
    mb voice release games/duel rocko --delete --yes  # and delete it from the account now, freeing the slot
    mb voice release games/duel --all --delete        # a dry run: says what it would delete
    mb voice say games/duel rocko t9 "Still got it!"  # recreates rocko for this batch, then deletes the recreation

- **Reference audio**: `mb voice pick` keeps the picked preview's own audio (as the provider returned it: no EQ, no loudness) in `art/voice_ref/<who>/preview.ogg`, and the first 4 lines the voice speaks join it (`line_<id>.ogg`). They're listed in `art/voices.toml` (`reference`, with each file's SHA-256) and committed with the game: about 8 KB a second. `release` makes sure some are there first; for a cast picked before this existed it takes them from the raw preview or the raw line takes in `art/raw/` if this checkout still has them, and refuses when there's nothing to keep.
- **Recreating**: `say`, `script` and `regen` on a released character speak with the original voice while it's still on the account. Once it's deleted, the first line of a batch recreates it with ElevenLabs Instant Voice Cloning (`POST /v1/voices/add`) from that reference audio, named `mb temp <game> <who>`; every line of the batch uses it, and it's deleted at the end (also when the batch fails). `--keep` leaves it on the account (it takes a slot) and records it, so later batches reuse it. A recreation needs a free slot for the length of the batch and a plan with Instant Voice Cloning (Starter and up), and each one counts as a voice added this month. mb only recreates from files it kept itself: a file in `art/voice_ref/` whose hash isn't the one recorded is refused, and the refusal of clones, impressions and real people's voices is unchanged.
- **The trade-off**: a recreation is very close to the designed voice, not identical: delivery and timbre shift a little between recreations. Speak a character's lines in one batch when you can (`mb voice script`), and respeak a scene's lines together if they sit side by side. A line's sidecar says `"voice": "recreated from art/voice_ref/<who>/…"` when it was spoken by a recreation; releasing doesn't make lines stale (their `key` is unchanged).

**Housekeeping.** `mb voice gc` lists the account's voices no game under the repository speaks with: released ones (their games keep them as audio), temporary recreations an interrupted batch left behind (`mb temp …`), and voices no game here uses at all, which can include voices you made outside mb. It's a dry run: it prints exactly what it would delete and why. `--yes` deletes exactly that list, and marks released voices as deleted in their games; `--except <id or name>` (repeatable) keeps one. It refuses when it finds no `art/voices.toml` (run from the wrong directory, everything would look unused) or can't read one, and stops at the first error that says the key lacks `voices_write`. Voices mb named for a game it doesn't find (`boltbrawl kai` when there's no `boltbrawl/` under here: a game in another checkout or on another branch) are listed in `slots` on their own and left alone by `gc` unless you add `--other-games`. Still, run it from the repository that holds all your games, on the branch that has them all, or pass every directory with games: any other voice a game it doesn't see uses counts as unused.

    mb voice gc                          # what it would delete
    mb voice gc --yes --except V123abc   # delete them, keeping one

**Premade voices take no slot.** When the account is full, or for minor characters, cast one of ElevenLabs' premade voices:

    mb voice premades                                   # all of them, with gender, age, accent and character
    mb voice premades female british                    # only those whose labels match every word
    mb voice cast games/duel announcer --premade Adam --delivery creative --style 0.6 --speed 1.1

`cast` takes a name (any case, or the start of one) or an id and writes the character into `art/voices.toml` with `premade = "<name>"`; `--delivery`, `--style`, `--speed` and `--model` become the character's defaults for `say` and `script` (a line's own flags still win). A character that had a designed voice keeps its description and reference audio; its old voice is no longer used here (`mb voice slots` lists it as unused). Premade voices are shared by everyone, so they're less distinctive than a designed one: give the character a strong delivery and style. `mb voice pick` switches a character back to a designed voice. `slots` and `release` ignore premade voices.

**Errors.** When ElevenLabs refuses to keep or recreate a voice because the account is full, mb says `no free voice slot: run mb voice slots` with the ways out (release, gc, a premade), instead of the raw API message; when the month's voice adds and edits are used up, it says that. A voice that's gone from the account (`voice_not_found`) makes a released character's batch recreate it.

**Key permissions.** Each ElevenLabs key has per-feature permissions, set at https://elevenlabs.io/app/settings/api-keys. A call the key isn't allowed returns an error naming the permission it lacks and where to turn it on:

| Command | Permissions |
|---|---|
| `design`, `pick` | Voice Generation (Text to Voice), plus `user_read` for the slot check (without it, a note and no check) |
| `say`, `script` | `text_to_speech`; for a released character also `voices_write` (recreate, then delete) and `user_read` (slot check) |
| `slots` | `user_read` (the count), `voices_read` (the list); either alone shows its half |
| `release` | none; `--delete --yes` needs `voices_write` |
| `premades`, `cast --premade` | `voices_read` |
| `gc` | `voices_read`; `--yes` needs `voices_write` |

## Skies (`mb art sky`)

An mb3d game can paint its sky with an equirectangular panorama (`gfx3d::sky_texture`, MB3D.md "Sky v2"): 2:1, the image's middle column straight ahead, its top row straight up and its bottom row straight down, the left and right edges one direction. Image models don't reliably draw that projection, so `mb art sky` asks for what they do draw well and makes the rest true:

- **The prompt** (it's the kind's framing; `--dry-run` shows it): "a seamless 360-degree equirectangular panorama for a 3D game's sky, like a photo sphere unwrapped", the horizon as one straight, level line across the exact vertical middle, the top and bottom edges straight up and down "so the top and bottom rows are each one even color", the left and right edges continuing into each other, everything far away with nothing in the foreground. Describe the sky and the distant horizon, not a scene in front of the camera: "a clear moonless night full of stars and a faint Milky Way over a distant silhouette of rooftops and pagodas with a few warm lantern lights". Leave the sun or moon out if the game draws one (`SkyDisc`), and clouds if it draws its own (`Clouds`). In our tries Nano Banana 2.1 drew the night prompt above as a convincing panorama on the first call: a level horizon (at 55% of the height, not 50%), a band of distant silhouettes, the sky above with stars to the top, still water below; not a true equirect (the Milky Way doesn't curve as a sphere's would), which only shows near the poles.
- **Aspect**: no provider makes 2:1, so it's asked for 21:9 (3168×1344 at 2K on Gemini) and cropped at the sides to 2:1 plus a 6% band (~10% of the width), keeping its pixels' aspect, then the band is joined onto the opposite edge where the two match best (as for layers), so the panorama wraps. A provider that returns something narrower than 1.6:1 gets a warning saying how much was cropped.
- **The horizon** goes on the middle row. `--horizon auto` (the default) finds it: the row where the picture changes most from above to below (mean brightness falling, each pixel's brightness changing, which catches a thin water line under distant hills where the averages hardly move, and saturation changing), lightly smoothed, between 25% and 85% of the height with a prior around 55%; the sidecar records how sure it was (`horizon_confidence`, 0–1: how far the best row stands out from the next best), and under 0.25, or with no horizon at all (then 0.5), it warns you to look at `art/preview` and pass `--horizon 0.55`, which sets it. `--span` is how many degrees of elevation the image covers: at 180 (the default) its top row is the zenith and its bottom row the nadir, and each half is stretched to its 90°; below 180 it covers that many degrees at an even scale (an ordinary wide view: `--span 60`), and the rows it doesn't reach are filled by stretching its top and bottom rows' colors toward the poles, blurring as they go. That's color extension, not painting: the warning and the sidecar's `extended_rows` say how many rows were made that way. There's no AI outpainting step.
- **The poles**: an equirect squeezes a whole row into one point at each pole, so any detail there pinches into a swirl in the 3D sky. Every row is blurred around the circle by how much it's squeezed (`(1/cos φ − 1)/2` pixels: nothing up to ~50°, ~2 px at 80°), and within `--poles` degrees (12) of each pole the rows blend into one even color, the mean of the cap's edge.
- **Output**: baseline JPEG (4:2:0) at the best quality from 88 down that fits `--max-kb` (800 KB by default): a 2048×1024 night sky is ~170 KB at quality 88, where a PNG would be several MB. The sidecar's `result` records the projection, where the source's horizon was (`source_horizon`, found or given), the crop, the extended rows and the JPEG quality; `mb art check` lists JPEGs with their size and flags any over mb3d's 2048² limit. In the game it's an ordinary texture (11 MB of the 96 MB texture budget at 2048×1024, decoded once when it loads) that's baked into the sky cube.

`mb regen <sky> --reprocess` redoes the mapping from the raw output for free (edit the sidecar's `recipe.horizon`, `span` or `poles` first to change them). The mock provider makes a 21:9 stand-in shaped like a real result (sky with stars to the top, hills on a horizon at the middle that don't wrap), which the tests turn into a seamless, pole-calm 2:1 JPEG.

## Originality and provenance

Prompts (and the style guide) are checked against a list of well-known characters, franchises and brands; a match is refused with a message explaining why (the community guidelines don't allow someone else's IP, and reviewers reject it). Named living artists and studios in "in the style of …" get a warning. A false positive, or IP you own, goes through with `--allow-name <word>`, and the sidecar records that. The list catches the obvious cases only; it doesn't clear anything.

Every generated asset has a sidecar, `<asset>.gen.json` (`assets/hero.png.gen.json`; the icon's is `icon.png.gen.json`): the generator and mb version, date, provider, model, the user's prompt and the full prompt sent, the seed and whether the provider honors seeds, reference files and the style guide with SHA-256 hashes, the estimated and actual cost (Gemini and Vertex don't report a charge, but bill these models per image or per call at a fixed price, so the actual cost is that list price for what was made, not counting the prompt's input tokens), the raw output's path and hash, the output's hash, names let through with `--allow-name`, any text the model returned, the resolved recipe `mb regen` replays, facts about the result (size, keyed color, loop points, BPM, loudness) and a provider note (SynthID). Sidecars are for `mb regen`, `mb art check` and audits, not the game, so they stay in the game's folder (commit them; the published source has them) and `mb build` leaves them out of the bundle, where they'd cost bytes of its 10 MB and an entry each of its 512 (Bolt Brawl's 199 came to 0.52 MB, 63 KB compressed). A game that reads them still gets them: code that names a `.gen.json` ships them as they are, code that names `provenance.json` ships them packed into one file, `assets/provenance.json` (bundle path of each asset → its sidecar). `mb build` says how many it left out and their size.

Files often get edited after they're generated: a script in the game brightens an effect, a sound editor trims one, a sprite is touched up by hand. `mb art check` notices (the file's hash isn't the sidecar's `output_sha256`), but by itself can only say "edited since it was generated". Say how with `mb art edited <asset> --tool <what> [--note "…"] [-- <its args>]`, after the edit; it appends to the sidecar's `derived` list:

```json
"derived": [
  { "tool": "tools/bright.py", "args": ["--shelf", "6"], "note": "a click on the attack",
    "date": "2026-10-09T16:10:45Z", "input_sha256": "a6f2…", "output_sha256": "1b53…" }
]
```

`tool` is a script's path in the game (so a reader can find it), another program, or `hand`; `input_sha256` is the file before (the generated output, or the previous edit's), `output_sha256` after, so edits chain. `output_sha256` at the top stays the generated file's. `mb art check` then says "edited after generation: tools/bright.py --shelf 6 (a click on the attack)"; a change after the last recorded edit is still flagged, and a file back to the generated bytes says its edits are undone. `mb regen` replaces the file, says which edits it had, and writes a sidecar without them: run them again and record them again. Edits mb itself makes (`--brighten`, `--reprocess` options) are in the recipe, not here.

`art/raw/` keeps every provider output (`<name>-<seed>.png|mp3|wav`), `art/variants/` the candidates, and `art/preview/` each image over a checkerboard, a dark and a light ground, enlarged: the file to look at. `mb art init` git-ignores all three; they're large and reproducible.

## Testing

`cargo test -p mb-cli` runs everything offline: image processing (keying and despill, pixel grids, palettes, quantizing, seamless wrapping, PNG encodings), audio (decoding, resampling, levels, tempo detection, loop cutting, layers, key and beat-phase detection, the tension kit's seams, key and grid, phone loss and `--brighten`, time-stretching and pitch-shifting; music in sections: a section played fast is slowed to the piece's tempo, one a semitone sharp is brought down, one three off is reported, and all are cut to one length and grid; the music graph: links between alike bars that keep the progression, a way back at each zone's end, the take's own transitions, sections on the piece's bars with their cues and segments; stingers landing on their mark; the SDK's Director playing the tension kit over a real take (`testdata/chase_take_excerpt.ogg`) at the level `mb music` writes, lifting it by at least +3 dB on a phone as mix_check measures it; Ogg encoding and `mb ogg` conversion), the IP check, keys, cost estimates, each provider's request bodies and response parsing against canned responses (for Tripo and Meshy, whole task sequences: create, poll, download, rig, clips, failures that keep the model), glTF reading and writing, simplifying, placement, textures, glow, clip merging and the preview renderer, and end-to-end runs of every command with the mock provider (`crates/mb-cli/tests/generate.rs`); the mock's 3D models are deterministic stand-ins shaped like a provider's (a fish or a four-legged critter, 23 k triangles, facing +z off the origin, an unsupported extension, a spine rig and one `.glb` per clip). mb-host's glTF tests load models `mb model` wrote (`crates/mb-host/src/mb3d/testdata/mb_model_koi.glb` with float geometry; `mb_model_koi_quantized.glb`, skinned, and `mb_model_crate_quantized.glb`, static, as it writes them now) and check the quantized ones come out the size and place mb reported. Which keys a command uses is tested against a fake Maimbrain server on localhost (`crates/mb-cli/tests/maimbrain_keys.rs`, and the unit tests in `generate/maimbrain.rs`); `MB_CONFIG_DIR` keeps the tests away from your real `mb login`.
